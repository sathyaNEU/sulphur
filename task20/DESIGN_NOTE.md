## Deep reasoning

Task type: Liquidity, funding & balance-sheet risk (the client's Quantitative Finance task type; the house's forecasting keys are strategy-backtesting-evaluation and volatility-derivatives-pricing, and liquidity-funding-balance-sheet is the niche)
Domain test: private-markets liquidity planning: unfunded commitments, the managers' deployment and short-term financing, exits and distributions in a stressed exit market, and fundraising timelines decide how much a long-horizon investor can commit; the results are the field's quantities (unfunded commitments, capital calls, distributions, the line-financed capital a fund carries, a commitment pace).
Swap: put the same call in a retailer's inventory or a bank's deposit book and the subscription lines, the recycling provision, exit-driven distributions and first closes stop making sense.
Where the weight sits: the capital already invested on credit (R2), rebuilt from three records at three grains and told apart from recycling, and the stressed distributions (R3), measured across episodes the endowment's own funds only partly saw.
Lesson: an investor's liquidity in a stress turns on cash its managers have already spent on its behalf on credit, on exits that answer the market late and recover slowly, and on fundraising that slows when the market does; the capital accounts show none of these until the cash moves, and the field's pacing model, fitted to them and checked on the last stress, passes its own back-test. No build on file teaches recovering a stock of line-financed capital from a fund's activity and audited borrowings.
