3. Step-by-step process.
Located and extracted the supplied archive, then read the two governing documents before touching scores. The stability standard, version 2, approved by the chief risk officer on 12 October 2026, defines the five model-side sources, excludes merchants' own change from them, sets 13-week consecutive snapshot pairs, weights families by their scored books at the latest quarter-end snapshot, makes the annual movement the root of the mean of the four pair mean-squares, sets the under-5.5 Monitor, 5.5-to-under-8 Remediation plan and at-least-8 Escalation bands, defines shares on mean-square movement, and directs the covenant counterfactual to remove plan and escalation sources in full. Schedule C, as amended 19 January 2026 and in force from 22 December 2025, sets tiers under 400, 400 to under 500, 500 to under 600 and 600-plus, sets Monday refresh every 13th week, and caps tier changes at 12% measured on the panel; the 2027 pairs are 21 December 2026 to 22 March, 21 June, 20 September and 20 December 2027.
Inventoried all 29 archive files. Used: model registry; scored books by family; monitoring-panel roster and ten quarterly score files; MRM pair register and pair scores; scheduler JSONL; retune log; releases log; 2027 roadmap; 2027 labelled-row plan including December 2027 book; warehouse rows; platform calendar; Schedule C monitor; both PDFs. Not used for the decision: weekly model performance, weekly feature-drift PSI and per-run training compute. The standard makes them neither a listed source nor an assessment input; inspecting them did not change any placement. Training-row volumes from the scheduler and warehouse were used.
Standardized panels and pairs. Loaded 3,465,865 panel score rows and collapsed them to 3,360,865 unique family-merchant-date records; where a date had restatement loads such as 09 December 2024, 11 August 2025, 20 April 2026 and 03 August 2026, the later load was kept. Mapped 4,568 Tolka Pay identifier pairs, covering 287,784 old-identifier score records and 288,781 new-identifier records, before pairing. The panel at 21 September 2026 has 23,943 merchants and the latest 23 November refresh has 23,436; leavers were not replaced. Pair scores covered 644 instruments and 1,125,752 merchant rows. Scheduler history had 2,464 runs.
Chose the 21 September 2026 scored book, 147,893 merchants, as the standard-literal latest quarter-end snapshot for a November 2026 assessment. December 2027 planned-book weights were retained as a sensitivity, not the primary.
Estimated each family's weekly instrument movements from pair mean-square score differences, weighting each refresh week equally. Replicate pairs estimate two refit draws, so one weekly refit's noise shock is half the replicate pair mean-square. Bootstrap pairs were converted to sensitivity K = (bootstrap pair mean-square minus replicate mean-square) times instrument-pair training rows; each planned week's data variance is K divided by that week's training rows and by its 52- or 104-week window. Release, retune and label pairs were used directly because regressing panel score jumps on pair effects gave slopes at about 1.00, whereas subtracting replicate noise from label or retune pairs can drive estimated effects negative. Future panel changes regressed on release effects gave slopes near zero from 1 to 13 weeks, and release, retune and label behave alike, so warm-started sources were treated as permanent 13-week random-walk accumulations rather than decaying effects.
Estimated actual platform adoption family-by-family from PC-24-031 runtime image, PC-24-047 warm start and PC-25-012 mixed precision by comparing each planned week with the weeks production runs actually used the target state. First-adoption lags run 3 to 34 weeks after the planned week and can include transient rollbacks. For every lag after a 2027 planned week, expected pinned probability is the fraction of the three prior changes running the target state then, with horizon beyond observation treated as pinned. This produced the family expected weeks in 04 Pinning 2027 and 429.33 of 728 retrains. Weighted per-family curve logic also feeds release eligibility.
Projected retunes by carrying each family's observed four-week cadence forward from its last 2026 refresh; this gives 13 retunes per family in the four pairs. Releases were read from roadmap target_refresh, not the filed quarter labels: FR-27-01 is filed Q4 but lands 28 December 2026 in the first 2027 pair, and FR-27-24 lands 20 December 2027 at the closing snapshot. Release movement counts only weeks a family is expected still to run live views. Rows use the 2027 plan plus warehouse history over each family's current window.
Built each family's four pair source variances, weighted them by the September scored book, and took the root of their four-pair mean for the annual movement. The resulting quarter model-side RMS values are 18.81, 17.36, 16.00 and 15.63 points (02 Quarterly movement). Applied the standard's bands and the mean-square shares, yielding the five placement rows above.
Computed merchants' own change under standard section 5.2: for each of the three 2026 pairs that closed, took panel mean-square change over merchants scored at both, subtracted model-side movement as it stood then using that year's actual releases, retunes, rows and live views, then carried the pooled pair residual into 2027 unchanged and weighted it at the September book. The result is 154.13 points-squared. This is reported apart, and its share of all movement is it divided by itself plus the five model-side mean-squares.
For tier movement, treated score change as Gaussian with zero mean and variance equal to the pair's expected mean-square movement, computed flip probabilities at the 400, 500 and 600 boundaries for each September 2026 panel merchant, and panel-count weighted the family rates. This is a probability-weighted expected rate, not a forecast headcount, and future attrition was not projected. The full scenario keeps all five sources; the counterfactual keeps only monitor-placed retunes plus merchants' own change.
Checked the tier rule against history: applying it to the three closed 2026 pairs reproduces panel-weighted monitor rates of 15.24, 14.43 and 14.78 predicted versus 15.26, 14.42 and 14.78 actual using observed pair variances, with larger family-level deviations, so no rate scaling was applied. The September panel baseline gives the headline 13.56/8.52; the latest 23 November refresh gives 13.48/8.49 (03 Covenant 2027 note).
Stress-tested the deciding choices. December 2027 planned-book weights move noise to 9.10 points and 28.78%, data to 6.53 and 14.82%, retunes to 4.47 and 6.95%, releases to 6.52 and 14.78%, labels to 9.99 and 34.68%, and merchants' own share to 34.71%; no band or the call changes. Deterministic mean first-adoption lag gives 61.13% pinned and releases at 6.38 points, 14.20%; median lag gives 63.05% and releases at 6.30 points, 13.89%; both leave releases in Plan. Treating a replicate pair mean-square as a single-refit shock rather than half makes noise 12.87 points and 44.55% and cuts merchants' own share to 16.09%; total tier movement is unchanged because the own residual absorbs it, and no band changes. These checks are in 08 Method & checks.
The method is expected-movement variance decomposition plus boundary probabilities, not a fitted merchant behavioural model: the standard defines movement as expected mean-square change, sources enter additively in that metric, and tier change depends only on the starting score and the change distribution. A Monte Carlo was unnecessary because Gaussian tier probabilities are determined by variance. Individual panel-lag regressions were used for persistence validation, but observed lag MS alone cannot isolate sources because releases, retunes, labels and data vary by week; the instruments exist precisely for that. Equal family weighting was rejected because section 3.1 requires scored-book weighting; annual averaging without quarter pairing was rejected because section 3.2 requires the four-pair mean.

4. Assumptions and judgment calls.
Latest quarter-end snapshot means 21 September 2026, because the assessment is made in November 2026 on records to 23 November and the 21 December 2026 snapshot has not closed. The supplied December 2027 book was treated as a planning sensitivity, not the standard's latest snapshot. A different reasonable weighting changes figures slightly but no band and not the recommendation.
Training data is interpreted as the rows a weekly refit sees, estimated through bootstrap sensitivity scaled to each planned week's actual window rows and window length, with shocks accumulating under warm starts. Bootstrap pairs measure sensitivity, not realised weekly draws; actual 2027 rows are a plan. A materially different data rule could move the 6.61-point figure, but this source is not near either band edge enough for the tested alternatives to alter the Plan call, and the limitation is disclosed.
Pinning is treated as family-specific expected adoption, averaging the three past changes at each lag, including rollbacks, and treating weeks beyond observed adoption as pinned. Only three historical changes exist per family, so these are committed planning estimates, not contractual behaviour. Mean or median lag does not change bands. Treating pins as effective in the planned week is the one tested choice that does change the call and is rejected below.
Release movement uses target refresh dates, not filed quarter labels, and zero contribution in pinned weeks per section 1.5.
Retune timing is assumed to continue the observed four-week cadence; no 2027 retune schedule exists in the inputs.
Replicate pair variance is halved for a single refit; effect pairs are used directly because panel jump slopes were about 1.00. Warm-started effects are permanent because future-change slopes were near zero.
Expected tier rates use a fixed September 2026 panel, Gaussian zero-mean changes, and a simple average across the four pairs; future attrition is not projected. Per-pair and family rates are shown so a reviewer does not have to accept only the average.
Merchants' own change is the pooled 2026 pair residual carried unchanged, per section 5.2, not a 2027 forecast of merchant behaviour.
These are committed estimates without sampling intervals, as section 1.1 directs; GAME has only about 270 to 287 panel merchants and is the least precise family.
The pinned-retrain headline counts every scheduled family-week retrain equally; the book-weighted alternatives are labelled separately and are not substituted for the retrain count.
5. Alternatives rejected.
Treating pins as effective in their planned weeks would cut releases to 4.00 points, 6.10% of model-side movement, making them Monitor and changing the call. Rejected because no historical change ran on its planned week and scheduler adoption trailed the planned week by 3 to 34 weeks, including transient rollbacks; actual adoption, not the calendar, is what section 1.5 keys on. Pinning context for slide 4 is the same evidence. A genuinely faster, evidenced PC-26-058 rollout would be the trigger to re-cut, and slide 5 states that tripwire.
Treating planned completion as adoption is rejected: the calendar says when an option was released to the training environment, while the question asks which retrains actually run pinned, and historical scheduler options changed weeks to months after planned weeks in the cases cited on 04 Pinning 2027.
Treating replicate pair mean-square as one refit's shock is rejected on the pair's construction: it is the difference of two refits from the same parent. The sensitivity leaves every band intact but would misleadingly cut merchants' own share to 16.09%.
Subtracting replicate noise from release, retune or label pairs is rejected because panel jumps on those weeks regress on pair effects at about 1.00, and for label and retune pairs such subtraction can produce negative source effects.
Decaying warm-start effects was rejected by the near-zero future-change slopes, and static quarterly snapshot-only panel analysis was rejected because it cannot attribute movement to sources.
Using the December 2027 planned book as primary is rejected for the standard-literal November 2026 assessment; it does not change any band and is disclosed as a sensitivity.
Ignoring pinning for releases, or treating roadmap quarter labels as timing, would overstate or misplace release movement; FR-27-01 demonstrates the label issue.
The strongest evidence against a simple "plans fix the covenant" reading is GAME: its monitor-only counterfactual is 12.66%, above the cap (05 Family tier detail), even though the all-family counterfactual is 8.52%. GAME's book weight is small, so it does not change the aggregate share or placements, but it should be flagged rather than read as every family clearing the cap. The September panel baseline also yields slightly higher rates than 23 November, and family-level 2026 tier calibration deviates more than the panel total; both are disclosed and neither changes the overall result.
Actual adoption uncertainty is pointed against the release placement: if the three historical rollouts are badly unrepresentative and 2027 pins land materially faster, releases fall toward the 4.00-point Monitor case. That is why the tripwire, not an unconditional release plan, is the operative recommendation; it does not change the call on available evidence.
