4. Assumptions and judgment calls.
The consequential judgment is bootstrap pricing. I used half of the between-bootstrap pair MS on the symmetric difference of consecutive windows. Reasons: consecutive windows replace old rows as well as add new ones; a bootstrap pair compares two resamples, so its MS is a two-model difference; and the weekly panel check favours this over added-rows-only face-value scaling. This is not settled by the standard, which defines movement but not instrument arithmetic. A different reasonable choice would change the call: added-rows-only face-value scaling yields Training data 7.1146 points → Remediation plan, total model-side movement 16.9870 points, shares noise 4.4158 / data 17.5413 / retunes 6.9330 / releases 36.5422 / labels 34.5676; Training data would then also enter §6.1 removal. It would not change the two escalations, but it would change the Training-data placement and the exact counterfactual. I therefore disclose it in workbook 06 and slide 5 rather than bury it.
Endpoint noise is carried once per pair, not multiplied by 13. §3.1 asks what noise alone does between two snapshot models; intermediate week seeds are not compared by those snapshots. A weekly-accumulation convention would materially inflate noise and is not how §3.2’s snapshot metric reads; it is a defensible alternative only under an explicit warm-start accumulation rule the standard does not state, and no filed figure supports one.
Instrument pair MS are used at face value, not stripped of replicate noise. Weekly panel scoring reproduces release/retune instrument effects at coefficients ≈1; subtracting replicate MS makes label-freeze and retune-shadow “effects” negative, which is not usable as a source movement. Bootstrap’s half factor is a pair-design factor, not a noise strip.
September 2026 book weights are used, not the December 2027 plan book. §1.1 fixes assessment on records to the latest weekly refresh and §3.1 says latest quarter-end snapshot; the latest closed one is 21 Sep 2026. December 2027 is a planning figure, not a snapshot. Using it (183,364 merchants) changes nothing structural: noise 3.5683 Monitor / 4.8956%; data 4.7193 Monitor / 8.5635%; retunes 4.4690 Monitor / 7.6791%; releases 10.2655 Escalation / 40.5181%; labels 9.9862 Escalation / 38.3437%; total 16.1270 points. No band changes.
Latest-quarter-end panel is the 2027 expectation base, with no attrition/replacement modelled because C4 does not replace leavers and no 2027 attrition schedule exists; “scored at both” is treated as the panel available at 21 Sep 2026. The four-pair share is reported as their mean, and each individual pair conclusion is identical (all over cap; all counterfactuals under), so a maximum/per-pair reading does not change the covenant conclusion.
Gaussian change with mean zero. Supported by the 2026 check above and by near-zero panel mean score changes; actual source changes have no filed signed drift, and §3 measures mean-square, not signed movement. A different change distribution could alter tier shares slightly at boundaries but not the cap conclusion given every pair is 2.08–2.58 pp over and every counterfactual 1.06–1.28 pp under.
Retunes projected at the historical 28-day family cadence, because no 2027 retune plan is filed; 13 per family per year, three/four per pair. If the retune job changes cadence, only the Retunes figure/share moves, not the escalation call (it would need to reach 5.5 from 4.4728).
Release target-refresh dates are used as first-scored snapshot membership, with (opening, closing]; FR-27-01 is included in 2027 despite its roadmap “Q4” label. Release effects are not scaled by number of feature groups because pair MS is flat across group counts within family.
Label revisions are not scaled by planned row volumes, because pair MS has no systematic within-family volume relationship; 13 weeks × pair MS per pair.
Pinned views are counted, not credited with a movement effect. PC-26-058 files a change, not a measured source-movement delta; there is no basis to reduce any source’s 2027 MS. Headline is calendar-2027; pair-window sensitivity is 90.9341%.
Committed estimates, not forecasts with intervals. §1.1 asks for committed estimates; I do not manufacture confidence intervals beyond the pair/panel diagnostics. Family residuals and source MS are point estimates.
2026 stability-plan trigger. All 42 closed monitor rows exceed 12%, so C3.2 requires a stability plan before 31 January 2027 showing expected next-year share. I treat 14.2887% as that filed expectation and the counterfactual as committee sensitivity, not as remediation already delivered. This is scheduling/compliance context, not an extra model-side source or placement.

Rejected on consecutive-window mechanics (old rows leave), two-resample pair design (factor ½), and weekly panel behaviour favouring half-symmetric scaling. Treating noise as 13 weekly refreshes of movement. Rejected: §3.2 is between endpoint snapshots and the replicate pair is exactly a seed-to-seed endpoint model difference; multiplying counts intermediate seeds the snapshots never compare. Removing training data as well as the escalated sources as the primary §6.1 counterfactual. Weighting feature releases by number/type of changed feature groups. Rejected: release-shadow MS stratified by group count is flat within every family (e.g. one vs two vs three groups move together; SUBS is uniformly high, PROF uniformly low).

Replacing family-specific instrument estimates with one pooled family estimate. Rejected: pair MS levels differ materially (replicate RMS ~2.97 PROF to ~5.09 GAME; bootstrap MS ~99.9 PROF to ~1,752 GAME per pair), and registry custom components/epochs vary; §3 weights families, it does not assert they share movement.
