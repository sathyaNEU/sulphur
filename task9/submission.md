## Step-by-Step

1. `risk_pnl_daily_legacy_20170103_20230630.csv` and `risk_pnl_daily_20230703_20261106.csv` are the risk system's daily P&L before and after the 3 July 2023 migration; the second states P&L in USD thousands and capital in USD millions. `book_code_map_20220401.csv` maps the pod codes re-issued on 1 April 2022, the legacy file carries 12 March 2021 twice (75 duplicate rows) and has no rows on 2018-08-07, 2020-02-19 and 2021-09-08, whose P&L sits in the next day's row. On one footing the record is 223 pods over 2,473 days, and each pod's month agrees with `kestrel_month_end_statements_201701_202610.csv` once the month-end true-ups are moved back a day.
2. Alpha (policy section 7) is P&L beyond a passive holding of the markets a pod trades, on the series the platform keeps. A holding earns its price move and its carry: the 10-year's yield over the bill, the Baa spread over the 10-year, a G10 basket's interest differential (long the three highest 3-month rates, short the three lowest, each leg earning its own rate). Each pod's daily return on capital is regressed on those holdings; a group's alpha is its pods' alpha P&L over their capital-years. Credit comes to 3.64%. The hurried analysis uses `Risk_Model_Methodology_2023.pdf`, whose factors carry only price moves: credit's specific P&L is 6.39%, the spread it earned left in. Monthly regressions, a lag of each factor, a two- or four-a-side carry basket and the group's own markets alone all land inside every band.
3. Capacity (policy 8e) is fitted as alpha per dollar falling by the same share at any size, alpha = a(K/150m)^-b, on pod-years with 120 or more trading days and a separate a for each pod: b = 0.425, 25.5% per doubling. Across pods (one a for all) b = 0.040, 2.7%: the committee gives more capital to stronger records (policy section 2), so across pods size and skill rise together. Weighting pod-years by days, pod-quarters, pod-years of 200 days or more, the onboarding steps alone and the first year after them give 22.6% to 24.7%.
4. Each pod's edge at USD 150 million is read from its own residual, each day weighted by its capital's capacity factor, and pulled toward its group's by how much the group's pods really differ beyond their records' noise (a normal prior per group). The draft raises the pods with the best three-year records, and part of a short record is luck: taken at face value the year shows USD 728 million.
5. The year (policy 8a to 8d): 10,000 simulated years, the markets drawn from the record a quarter at a time with their means set to zero, each pod at its 2027 capital (onboarding pods on their schedule) with its loadings and its own noise, halved or closed by the drawdown rules, funded at 4.01% plus 0.35% on a fifth of its capital, paid 18% of its own positive year, less platform costs of USD 218 million: USD 594 million, the 10th percentile USD 37 million and the 90th USD 1,077 million. Capacity read on pod-quarters, pod-years weighted by days or the onboarding steps, priors fitted by maximum likelihood or with one spread for all groups, and markets drawn a month or half a year at a time all land inside the bands. Drawing the markets a day at a time loses credit's weeks-long runs and puts the 10th percentile at USD 115 million; on the risk model's factors the year is USD 722 million; taking each pod's record at face value gives USD 728 million and leaving the drawdown rules out USD 647 million. The careful but hurried analysis, on the risk model's factors with days drawn one at a time, gets USD 712 million, USD 244 million to USD 1,180 million, and with the carry netted alone still USD 115 million to USD 1,054 million.
6. The hurdle (policy section 9): each increase is run at its 2026 and its 2027 capital on the same simulated years, and the difference in its expected net P&L after payout and funding is divided by the capital it adds. HC1054, HC1058, HC1062, HC1082, HC1098, HC1107, HC1132 and HC1135 come to 1.11%, 0.93%, 0.76%, 0.99%, 0.82%, 0.33%, 0.38% and 0.96%. Judged instead on the pod's net return on its whole new capital, which is not what the added dollar earns once alpha per dollar falls with size, HC1107 and HC1132 alone fail. On the risk model's factors HC1054, HC1058, HC1062, HC1082 and HC1135 clear the hurdle, and with each pod's record taken at face value HC1098 clears it; read across pods, no increase fails.
7. `allocation_plans_2018_2026.xlsx` shows what the house pricing has done before: each plan since 2018 expected more than the pods it listed went on to earn in 7 of the 8 completed years, by USD 374 million a year on average (those pods' net P&L for the year in `kestrel_month_end_statements_201701_202610.csv`, payouts included, less the year's platform costs; pods hired during the year left out).

No other plan survives the files. The policy plans alpha with the markets at zero, so a pod's return on capital, which carries the market and carry returns it rode, is not what a pod is expected to earn, and the past plans priced that way ran ahead of their pods in 7 years of 8. Credit's spread is what a passive holding earns, so it cannot be a credit pod's alpha; capital has always followed records, so only the pods' own histories show what size costs; a record of a few years is part luck, and the draft rewards the luckiest; and the drawdown rules and pod-by-pod payouts are part of the year the policy plans. Every sound way of reading each of those lands the plan between 549 and 649 and the same eight increases below the hurdle.
