## Step-by-Step

1. Assemble and clean the record. Keep the later load_id on the four re-sent weekly refreshes across the ten quarterly panel CSVs and map Tolka Pay's re-keyed merchant identifiers through tolka_pay_merchant_id_migration.csv, producing 25,007 unique panel merchants (28,854 identifiers before mapping) and 2,845,101 scored merchant-weeks (2,933,990 raw rows). Deduplicate training_scheduler_runs_2024_2026.jsonl on run_id (2,867 lines, 2,858 distinct runs, 1,764 production refits), reconcile the renamed rows / train_rows field across the runtime image change, map scheduler job families to registry codes via gms_model_registry.csv, and align ISO weeks with Monday dates. The 2026 instrument register contains 870 pairs (84 replicate, 224 bootstrap, 168 retune shadow, 282 release shadow, 112 label freeze). Book weights follow section 3 of the stability standard at the latest closed quarter-end snapshot (21 September 2026, 125,031 scored merchants). Leaving any single hygiene step undone keeps every graded figure inside its band.
2. Baseline forced sources (retunes and label revisions). Compute each family's mean pair variance from mrm_instrument_pair_scores_2026.csv and scale by snapshot-pair frequency (one retune every fourth Monday, 3.25 per pair; thirteen weekly label revisions per pair). Book weighted, retunes contribute 18.42 points squared (4.29 points, monitor) and label revisions contribute 96.34 points squared (9.82 points, escalation). Every route lands both placements.
3. Optimisation noise (Component 1, warm-start persistence). Section 1 of the standard notes that warm-started refits carry prior training randomness, and the replicate pairs measure fresh single-refit spread (book-weighted mean-square 22.62 points squared). Treating thirteen weekly refits as independent permanent shocks gives 12.13 points (half a replicate pair per refit, escalation) or 17.15 points (a full replicate pair per refit, escalation), while reading a single replicate pair at face value gives 4.76 points (monitor). Instead, measure each family's mean-square score change D_warm(h) across lags h = 1 to 25 over its settled warm weeks (starting 13 weeks after each family's last cold-start week, or October 2025 where later). Every family's curve is strongly concave: straight lines fitted on lags 8 to 25 cross lag 0 at 75.5 points squared book weighted (at least 41.3 in every family). To verify that this concavity comes from warm-start decay rather than mean-reverting merchant risk, inspect each family's cold-start era (at least 29 weeks before warm-start adoption, range 29 to 55 weeks): D_cold(h) across lags 1 to 14 is linear (mean relative quadratic curvature -0.010, range -0.053 to +0.018), ruling out mean-reverting merchant change from the data alone. Fitting D_warm(h) - D_data(h) = b*h + A*(1 - phi^h) with 1/D^2 weights across lags 1 to 25, anchored to each family's replicate pair variance A*(1 - phi^2) = rep, gives persistence phi from 0.64 to 0.82 and 13-week optimisation noise A*(1 - phi^13) of 6.86 points (band 6.56 to 7.25, remediation plan). Without subtracting the smooth data curve, the anchored fit remains 6.68 points (inside the band), confirming independence from Step 4. Fitting the raw warm curve unanchored absorbs the data curve's bend and overshoots to 9.20 points (implied replicate 33.12 against 22.62 measured).
4. Training data (Component 2, recency-weighted turnover). Section 1 of the standard and the scheduler manifests (options.sample_weights = recency_decay on 52-week and 104-week windows, versus uniform on 26-week cold windows) state that long-window refits apply exponential recency weighting and that bootstrap pairs resample with those production weights, without listing the decay factor. Taking raw bootstrap variance at face value gives 10.21 points (9.04 net of replicate, both escalation), while ignoring recency weighting and applying uniform-window turnover (entering + leaving)/(2N) drops training data to 3.65 points (monitor). Under exponential recency weighting across a W-week window ending at week t, week u carries normalised weight share p_u(lambda) = n_u * lambda^(t - u) / sum_v (n_v * lambda^(t - v)), so across each family's 16 bootstrap pairs in 2026 net bootstrap variance Var_net(f, t) = 2 * row2_f * sum_u (p_u(lambda)^2 / n_u) swings sharply in phase-lagged anti-phase with the trailing labelled row waves in warehouse_labelled_rows_weekly.csv. Inverting lambda_f and row2_f by matching log net bootstrap variance across the 16 dates yields lambda_f from 0.906 to 0.942 and cuts mean log-RMSE from 0.1881 (uniform lambda = 1) and 0.4947 (lag-0 1/n_t) down to 0.0495 (3.8x better than uniform). Alternative smooth recency kernels fitted to the same 16 pairs keep training data in remediation (shifted power-law (1 + age/g)^(-2) 7.29, linear ramp 6.22) while exponential achieves the lowest log-RMSE. Evaluating the exact weighted-window turnover variance row2_f * sum_u (p_{t1, u} - p_{t0, u})^2 / n_u across the four 2027 snapshot spans in labelled_row_plan_2027.xlsx yields 6.83 points (band 6.70 to 6.87, remediation plan).
5. Feature releases and pinned feature views (Component 3, within-group merchant covariance). First, model actual pinned-view take-up from the three completed rollouts (PC-24-031, PC-24-047, PC-25-012) by pairing platform_change_calendar.xlsx against first actual use and 26-week post-adoption reverts in the scheduler manifests. Fitting log-lag from environment completion to first actual use on each family's custom_training_components (validated leave-one-rollout-out: runtime 35.7 vs 36.1 per cent, warm start 37.5 vs 35.4, bf16 34.6 vs 37.3) and evaluating the log-normal adoption curve from planned week plus mean calendar slip scaled by (1 - mean revert share) over the 52 assessment-year Mondays (28 December 2026 to 20 December 2027, section 2 of the standard) gives 34.8 per cent of 2027 retrains on pinned views (band 32.4 to 38.3), against 83.4 per cent on calendar planned weeks and 79.5 per cent on environment-release slip alone. Second, inspect the merchant-level signed score deltas (score_b - score_a) across the 2026 release_shadow pairs in mrm_instrument_pair_scores_2026.csv joined to feature_platform_release_log.csv via excluded_release. Single-release variance is flat across groups (book-weighted mean 6.15 points squared), and 2026's semi-annual releases were spaced 27 weeks apart per group so weekly panel regressions see no clustering. At the merchant level, however, every pair of releases modifying the same feature group moves the same merchants in the same direction: across all 141 same-group pairs in 2026, pairwise correlation is strictly positive (mean 0.783, minimum 0.702), whereas the 2580 cross-group pairs average -0.001. In 2027, feature_platform_roadmap_2027.xlsx packs multi-phase sprints (three phased releases per priority group per quarter-pair). Evaluating each family's pair variance sum_k P_live(t_k) * v_f + 2 * sum_{k < l, same group} P_live(max(t_k, t_l)) * c_f * v_f yields 7.06 points (band 6.84 to 7.17, remediation plan). Treating releases as orthogonal drops feature releases to monitor at 4.70 points (1.96 on calendar planned dates), while treating all releases in a pair as perfectly aligned regardless of group pushes them to escalation at 11.10 points.
6. Internal 2026 record falsification check. Without observing any 2027 target, test every route against three 2026 statistics in native points squared: (i) settled warm 1-week panel variance D_warm(1) = 47.14 (committed model implies 47.06), (ii) high-minus-low trailing-volume bootstrap net-variance contrast = 10.88 (committed model implies 10.43), and (iii) same-group release-shadow cross-release merchant covariance = 4.80 (committed model implies 4.79). Summing the five independent sources' expected mean-square movements per snapshot pair (section 3 of the standard) yields combined model-side movement of 16.89 points in Q1, 17.07 in Q2, 15.52 in Q3, 14.69 in Q4, and 16.07 points across the assessment year (root of the four pairs' mean square).
