## Final Recommendation

Plan on about 2,483 drive replacements from October 2026 to September 2027 (80 per cent interval 2,266 to 2,713), rising from 553 in the fourth quarter to 701 in the third quarter of 2027, and migrate vaults B16, B17, B18 and B19 this quarter, leaving B15 to run: the HX-8 drives built in weeks 16 to 30 of 2024 are failing at an accelerating rate and fill those vaults, while B15 holds only 387 of them and its own replacements over 24 months (146) fall short of the 240 a migration needs; migration stays the cheaper choice unless the batch's rate doubles more slowly than every 24.1 months. Rejected: the close-out's rates carried forward (about 1,861 replacements and no migration), blaming the HX8.2.0 firmware (which takes in 988 drives that are not failing, migrates all five vaults and moves the flip point to 18.1 months), and migrating every vault on the batch's average.

## Step-by-Step

1. Assemble the replacements: a data drive's removal for FAIL or PULL, with blank reasons read from the technicians' tickets, reseats (the serial back within 30 days) merged into one service life, DECOM removals, boot SSDs and the QA-01 vault left out, duplicate telemetry rows dropped. Counting every removal in the age curve, the P2 harvest of healthy SMART 187 drives in June to August 2025 included, gives 3,020.
2. Rebuild DC-3's ages: every migrated drive's install date is 2024-03-01. Take Brightstore's manifest deploy date where it has one, the VB-12 drives' power-on hours at their first quarter, and otherwise the serial's build week plus the median deploy lag of Halden's own HX-8 drives (67 days); each drive then enters the hazard fit at its rebuilt age on 2024-03-01, so the failures Brightstore never passed on are not counted as survival.
3. Fit each family's hazard by age on drive-days (monthly bands in the first year, half-yearly after), with one infant term for the fleet (its decay chosen by likelihood, 2 months) and each family's age slope shrunk to the fleet's; health pulls of healthy drives under P2 are a separate rate (0.0044 per drive-year since September 2025), not wear-out.
4. Fit each vault's own level as a gamma frailty on its observed against expected replacements. The OM-6 reviews retired the vault with the most failures each time, and KS-10's review did the same, so the survivors sit below their family's curve: 281 OM-6 replacements, not the 345 one pooled curve gives.
5. Find the batch: scan the HX-8 drives' replacements over the last year by build week against each vault's own earlier level. The run of weeks 16 to 30 of 2024 stands out (likelihood ratio 46 against the rest); the HX8.2.0 builds of weeks 10 to 15 do not, so the cause is the build window, not the firmware. Fit the ramp in months since build as an exponential, a linear and a power shape and weight them by likelihood (0.35, 0.52 and 0.13).
6. Forecast by posterior predictive simulation: draw each family's curve, each vault's frailty and the ramp's shape, onset and slope, run the drives in service forward quarter by quarter (each replacement used up and its spare starting young), add the scheduled X-24 vaults, and draw Poisson counts (with each vault's level drawn, the vault-quarter residuals show no spread beyond it). The path is 553, 587, 642 and 701, 2,483 in all with an 80 per cent interval of 2,266 to 2,713.
7. Back-test the method: build the same intervals from the record to 30 September 2025 and score every family-quarter of the following year in the vaults that stayed in service: 78.1 per cent coverage. The batch was not yet visible at that cut, so the HX-8 cells are the misses; Poisson intervals cover 59.4 per cent and the careful-but-hurried method 75.0 per cent.
8. Decide: the fourth quarter's spare order per family is its 90th percentile less stock (402 in all). A vault pays for its migration when its own replacements over the 24 months from 1 October 2026 exceed 240 (USD 60,000 over USD 250 each); the batch would lose 36.3 per cent of its drives in that time, but the vaults hold it unevenly, so the call is made vault by vault: B15 146 (run to failure); B16 309 (migrate); B17 387 (migrate); B18 414 (migrate); B19 346 (migrate). A vault made wholly of the batch stops paying only if the batch's rate doubles more slowly than every 24.1 months.
9. Why nothing else survives: the close-out's rates carried give 1,861 and no migration, with DC-3 at 634; the batch inside its family gives 296 of its replacements and migrates B16; the firmware story counts 5,148 drives at 6.8 per cent, migrates all five vaults and moves the flip point to 18.1 months; deciding every vault on the batch's average migrates B15 too; one curve over every vault prices the OM-6 survivors at 345; and Poisson bands are 130 wide and covered 59 per cent of the back-test cells. Each misses a different figure, and every defensible variant of the golden (the infant term over two or four months, unshrunk slopes, either ramp shape alone, the build window a week narrower, DC-3's lag from the mean) migrates the same vaults; a week wider is ruled out by the week-15 builds, which fail at the family's normal rate, and would wrongly put B15 over the line.
