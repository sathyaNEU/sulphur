## Step-by-Step

1. From `CFF_VolPremium_Blotter.xlsx`, with the superseded August 2023 ticket (booked at USD 250,000) and the original of the confirmation novated in June 2024 left out, the sleeve is 128 confirmations, 104 settled and 24 open in twelve sale months at USD 50,000 a point a month; `Master_Confirmation_SPX_Variance_Swap.pdf` gives the formula for realized volatility, the cap and the settlement amount, and `SPX_History.csv` the closes.
2. `Larkhill_DealerValuations_Daily.csv` and the three dealer statements show how an open swap is valued: variance realized to date and variance implied to expiration, weighted by days, with an implied figure that differs swap by swap with the remaining term. On the November 9 closes of the Cboe volatility indices, implied volatility read in total variance between published maturities, the open swaps are worth USD 2,619,000; valuing every swap at the 1-year index it was struck on gives 2,452,000, which does not match the dealers.
3. Schedule D.5(b) in `IPS_Schedule_D_Volatility_Premium_Sleeve.pdf` keeps every monthly sale date from January 2007 in the record whether or not a ladder's index was yet published, and `VIX3M_History.csv` starts on September 18, 2009, so 32 strikes of the 3-month ladder have to be rebuilt. The hurried analysis regresses the 3-month index on the 30-day index, sees an R-squared of 0.94 over the 4,312 days both are published, and uses it: USD 300,000 a sale and 14,125,000 for 2008. Its check is the wrong one. On the 102 days the 30-day index closed above 35 the 3-month index runs 7.9% below it, against 12.5% above on average, and the stand-in is 2.3 points high there. Rebuilt by least squares on the 30-day index and the next longer published index (the 6-month from January 2, 2008, the 1-year before that), the fit is off by 0.43 points on average and 1.35 on those days, and the ladder averages USD 284,000 a sale with a worst year of 2008 at 16,154,000. Leaving the early sale dates out, which D.5(b) does not allow, gives 365,000 a sale and a worst year of 2020 at 5,036,000. The index's earlier closes are public but not in the folder; a solver who brings them in gets 286,000 and 16,166,000, inside the same bands.
4. The 12-month ladder is sold on the record at USD 50,000 a point at the 1-year index, and its open swaps are valued together each day on the dealers' basis with exposure capped as `CSA_Summary_Volatility_Sleeve.pdf` caps it: the peak is USD 25,876,000 (March 18, 2020, with November 20, 2008 at 25,287,000). Valuing each swap at the 1-year index gives 21,774,000, leaving the cap out of the valuation 30,067,000, and the worst calendar year of settlements is only 11,415,000; none is the collateral the policy measures.
5. Both ladders are run together on every day of the record against both limits (USD 44,628,000 of collateral and USD 29,752,000 of settlements, 3.0% and 2.0% of the USD 1,487.6 million in `CFF_Statement_of_Net_Assets_FY2026.pdf`). The binding day is November 20, 2008: the 12-month ladder stands at 25,287,000 against the sleeve, leaving 19,341,000 of room, and the 3-month ladder stands at 16,900,000 per USD 100,000 a point, so the largest size in multiples of USD 100 is 114,400. The settlement limit alone would allow 157,900. The stand-in's strikes give 124,500, the new ladder sized on its own 184,100, each swap valued at its own index 144,400, and the same run for a 6-month ladder 58,700, bound on the same day; the 6-month index is published from January 2, 2008, so every 6-month swap open that day has a published strike.
6. Schedule D.7(d) asks for the range of each average. A sale's settlement is tied to the next one's (correlation 0.93 for the 12-month ladder, 0.64 for the 3-month), because swaps sold a month apart share most of their days. Resampling the 226 and 235 settlements one at a time, as the hurried analysis does, gives USD 228,000 to 400,000 for the 12-month ladder and 194,000 to 370,000 for the 3-month. Resampling the record in blocks of consecutive sales twice the swap's life gives 63,000 to 552,000 and 143,000 to 413,000; other block lengths, a standard error that allows for at least the months neighboring swaps share, calendar-year averages and a count of only the swaps that do not overlap all land inside the bands. A standard error that allows for 5 lags, where neighboring 12-month swaps share 11 months, is still too narrow (123,000 to 507,000).
7. Schedule D.7(a) carries the open swaps at the settlement amounts they are expected to pay, and D.7(c) takes experience from the whole record. The ten sale months that expire in 2027 are valued at USD 1,883,000, and the hurried analysis budgets that. Taking each of the 4,761 start dates from January 3, 2007 to December 3, 2025, realized variance over each swap's remaining days as a share of the variance implied that day for the same term, applied to the November 9 term structure, the same swaps go on to pay more than their value from 80% of start dates and USD 2,494,000 on average, and between USD 394,000 and USD 4,247,000 four times in five when all ten are taken from the same start date. Realized variance has averaged 0.83 of implied over one month and 0.67 over twelve on the record. A forecast made in volatility and then squared gives 3,015,000, too high, because the settlement amount is linear in variance; the median start date (3,545,000) is not the expectation; and the sleeve's own years alone (3,312,000) are what D.7(c) rules out.
8. Schedule D.7 carries every swap that expires in the plan year. For the 12-month ladder that is the ten open sale months and the sales of November 23, 2026 and December 21, 2026, not yet made at the review date, which expire on November 19, 2027 and December 17, 2027: USD 315,000 at the ladder's average of USD 158,000 a sale (D.7(b)), so USD 2,809,000 for the ladder. With `NYSE_Holidays_2026_2028.csv` the new ladder's sales run from January 19, 2027 and only the nine to September 20 expire inside 2027 (April 16 to December 17; the June expiry moves to the 17th because the exchange is closed on the 18th): nine sales at USD 114,400 a point and the record's average bring USD 2,919,000. The plan is USD 5,728,000. Leaving out the two sales still to come gives 2,494,000 for the 12-month ladder, and twelve settlements for the new ladder would give 3,893,000.
9. The plan's range (D.7(e)) takes all 21 settlements from the same start date: the open swaps over their remaining days, and each swap not yet sold struck and run the same number of days after it, for 4,717 start dates to October 1, 2025. The 10th and 90th percentiles are minus USD 4,203,000 and USD 12,204,000, and the plan is below zero from 11% of start dates. Drawing the swaps not yet sold one at a time from their ladder's record gives minus 686,000 to 10,434,000, and drawing all 21 settlements independently 498,000 to 9,763,000; both miss that one bad stretch hits the open swaps and the new sales together.

No other size survives the files. The USD 200,000 quoted for would have put USD 59,087,000 of collateral on the sleeve on November 20, 2008, 14,459,000 over the limit. Schedule D.5(b) keeps the sale dates of 2007 to 2009 in the record, so the 3-month ladder cannot be run only where its index file has a close, and a stand-in built on the 30-day index alone overstates the missing strikes on exactly the stressed days that set the limit, which the days all the indices are published show. Every way of rebuilding those strikes from both neighboring maturities, and every way of reading the term structure between published maturities, binds on the collateral limit on November 20, 2008 and lands between 100,000 and 117,000; sizing the new ladder on its own, valuing swaps at the index they were struck on or leaving the cap out of the valuation each contradicts Schedule D.5 or the credit support annexes. For the plan, the value of an open swap is a price and not what Schedule D.7(a) asks for, and every estimate of the expectation drawn from the whole record lands between 2,390,000 and 2,890,000; the ranges have to respect that neighboring sales and the open swaps move together, which the settlements themselves show.
