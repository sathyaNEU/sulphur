## Step-by-Step

1. The requests come from Tidewell's audit in two generations (tidewell_rfq_audit_brindlemoor_20230103_20250404.csv to 4 April 2025 in New York time with quantities in face; tidewell_rfq_audit_brindlemoor_20250407_20261106.csv from 7 April 2025 in UTC with quantities in thousands and the outcomes coded), each request kept at its latest message version with re-sent messages counted once; 144A bonds are joined to their registered successors through the security master; a request's segment is its issuer's rating band on its date, its bond's bucket for its quarter and its client's type.
2. The auto-quoter's distance is the half-spread times multiples the schedule states, so a request's distance at another schedule is the distance shown times the ratio of the half-spreads; the current schedule is version 3 and the draft changes twelve segments.
3. Whether a request would be won at another quote is read from the level it traded at: the cover on the requests the desk won, and on the requests it lost the winning dealer's print on the public tape (a customer trade in the same bond on the same side within three minutes, of the same size or capped; cancelled reports dropped and corrected ones used), which matches 99.9% of them. A win model fitted on the desk's own quotes puts the draft's win shares far lower, because its quotes widen when the rivals' do.
4. A trade's cost after it is done (policy 5b) is the bond's evaluated price 30 trading days on, net of the Treasury curve's move and of the median move in the evaluated spreads of other issuers' bonds of its rating; 25 and 45 days give the same answers. Ashcote takes a medium or thin bond's own move in mostly at its month-end review, so the desk's next-day markouts and a week's marks miss much of what the requests that pick off stale evaluations cost.
5. Each schedule's 2027 P&L is the record since January 2023 replayed at its quotes (edge less cost less the offload charge on each trade won), each segment carried at its requests in the twelve months to October 2026, which gives USD 5.24 million at the current schedule and USD 10.30 million at the draft. The difference by changed segment is the bridge; liquid investment-grade bonds for asset managers, medium investment-grade bonds for asset managers, medium investment-grade bonds for hedge funds and thin investment-grade bonds for hedge funds lose money.
6. The year's range draws 251 trading days from the record in runs of 21 days (4,000 simulated years): USD 5.71 million to USD 15.01 million; runs of 10, 42 and 63 days land inside the bands. Days drawn one at a time give USD 7.68 million to USD 13.02 million, too narrow: the draft's daily P&L has a first-order autocorrelation of 0.19 (the bound for independent days is 0.06) and its 21-day sums vary 3.0 times what independent days would, because informed flow and pick-offs cluster around issuer moves and stress clusters.
7. No other conclusion survives: rivals' levels with a week's marks and the added trades costed like today's wins plan USD 11.80 million and drop only liquid investment-grade bonds for asset managers, medium investment-grade bonds for asset managers and medium investment-grade bonds for hedge funds; with each added request costed on its own record a week's marks plan USD 14.67 million and drop only liquid investment-grade bonds for asset managers and medium investment-grade bonds for hedge funds; the next-day view drops only liquid investment-grade bonds for asset managers and plans USD 22.15 million; a win model on the desk's own quotes drops liquid investment-grade bonds for asset managers, medium investment-grade bonds for asset managers, medium investment-grade bonds for hedge funds, thin investment-grade bonds for asset managers and thin investment-grade bonds for hedge funds and plans USD 6.54 million; complete costs with the added trades costed like today's wins plan USD 6.55 million. Only the replay on the rivals' levels with complete costs, each added trade on its own record, drops exactly liquid investment-grade bonds for asset managers, medium investment-grade bonds for asset managers, medium investment-grade bonds for hedge funds and thin investment-grade bonds for hedge funds.
