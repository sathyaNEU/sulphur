## Step-by-Step

1. `risk_pnl_daily_legacy_20170103_20230630.csv` and `risk_pnl_daily_20230703_20261106.csv` are the risk system's daily P&L before and after the 3 July 2023 migration; the second states P&L in USD thousands and capital in USD millions. `book_code_map_20220401.csv` maps the pod codes re-issued on 1 April 2022, the legacy file carries 12 March 2021 twice (74 duplicate rows) and has no rows on 2018-08-07, 2020-02-19 and 2021-09-08, whose P&L sits in the next day's row. On one footing the record is 220 pods over 2,473 days, and each pod's month agrees with `kestrel_month_end_statements_201701_202610.csv` once the month-end true-ups are moved back a day.
2. Alpha (policy section 7) is P&L beyond a passive holding of the markets a pod trades, on the series the platform keeps. A holding earns its price move and its carry: the 10-year's yield over the bill, the Baa spread over the 10-year, a G10 basket's interest differential (long the three highest 3-month rates, short the three lowest, each leg earning its own rate). Each pod's daily return on capital is regressed on those holdings; a group's alpha is its pods' alpha P&L over their capital-years. Credit comes to 4.31%. The hurried analysis uses `Risk_Model_Methodology_2023.pdf`, whose factors carry only price moves: credit's specific P&L is 6.42%, the spread it earned left in. Monthly regressions, a lag of each factor, a two- or four-a-side carry basket and the group's own markets alone all land inside every band.
3. Capacity (policy 8e) is fitted as alpha per dollar falling by the same share at any size, alpha = a(K/150m)^-b, on pod-years with 120 or more trading days and a separate a for each pod: b = 0.440, 26.3% per doubling. Across pods (one a for all) b = 0.075, 5.1%: the committee gives more capital to stronger records (policy section 2), so across pods size and skill rise together. Weighting pod-years by days, pod-quarters and the onboarding steps alone give 25.8% to 26.3%.
4. Each pod's edge at USD 150 million is read from its own residual, each day weighted by its capital's capacity factor, and pulled toward its group's by how much the group's pods really differ beyond their records' noise (a normal prior per group). The draft raises the pods with the best three-year records, and part of a short record is luck: taken at face value the year shows USD 776 million.
5. The year (policy 8a to 8d): 10,000 simulated years, the markets drawn from the record a quarter at a time with their means set to zero, each pod at its 2027 capital (onboarding pods on their schedule) with its loadings and its own noise, halved or closed by the drawdown rules, funded at 4.01% plus 0.35% on a fifth of its capital, paid 18% of its own positive year, less platform costs of USD 218 million: USD 665 million, the 10th percentile USD 270 million and the 90th USD 999 million. Capacity read on pod-quarters, pod-years weighted by days or the onboarding steps, priors fitted by maximum likelihood or with one spread for all groups, and markets drawn a month or half a year at a time all land inside the bands. Drawing the markets a day at a time loses credit's weeks-long runs and puts the 10th percentile at USD 352 million; on the risk model's factors the year is USD 720 million; taking each pod's record at face value gives USD 776 million and leaving the drawdown rules out USD 733 million. The careful but hurried analysis, on the risk model's factors with days drawn one at a time, gets USD 733 million, USD 414 million to USD 1,049 million, and with the carry netted alone still USD 352 million to USD 996 million.
6. The hurdle (policy section 9): each increase is run at its 2026 and its 2027 capital on the same simulated years, and the difference in its expected net P&L after payout and funding is divided by the capital it adds. HC1051, HC1059, HC1090 and HC1124 come to 0.43%, 0.79%, 0.12% and 0.66%. Judged instead on the pod's net return on its whole new capital, which is not what the added dollar earns once alpha per dollar falls with size, HC1051 and HC1090 fail. On the risk model's factors HC1059 clears the hurdle, and with each record at face value HC1124 does; read across pods, only HC1051 fails.
7. `allocation_plans_2018_2026.xlsx` shows what the house pricing has done before: each plan since 2018 expected more of its pods than they went on to earn in 7 of the 8 completed years, by USD 293 million a year on average (`kestrel_month_end_statements_201701_202610.csv`, net of payouts, less each year's platform costs).

No other plan survives the files. The policy plans alpha with the markets at zero, so a pod's return on capital, which carries the market and carry returns it rode, is not what a pod is expected to earn, and the past plans priced that way ran ahead of their pods in 7 years of 8. Credit's spread is what a passive holding earns, so it cannot be a credit pod's alpha; capital has always followed records, so only the pods' own histories show what size costs; a record of a few years is part luck, and the draft rewards the luckiest; and the drawdown rules and pod-by-pod payouts are part of the year the policy plans. Every sound way of reading each of those lands the plan between 643 and 686 and the same four increases below the hurdle.
