## Deep reasoning

Task type: interpretability (feature attribution, probing, mechanistic analysis), the task type the taxonomy has serve Root-Cause Analysis in this subdomain; the attribution here is of a deployed model's prediction churn to its sources
Domain test: every result is a quantity the statistics of model evaluation estimates. What a source moves a prediction over a stated gap is a variance component of prediction change. Training-window turnover is the variance of the difference between two estimators fitted on overlapping samples. Optimisation noise at the gap is the autocovariance of training noise across successive warm-started fits. The answer is the attribution of prediction churn across its sources, with the part that is the merchants' own change shown as the remainder and the whole checked against the score history at another gap. A careful statistician would defend each step as a statement about how a refitted estimator varies.
Swap: with a hospital's nouns (a ward's readmission risk scores moving between quarterly audits, split among five causes) a replicate of one refit, a bootstrap of a sliding training window, a warm start that inherits last week's weights and a pinned feature view have no object; the three estimators are statements about how a retrained model's predictions vary and about nothing else.
Where the weight sits: R3, R4 and R5. Each is its own estimator on its own records for fourteen families: the rows that leave and enter a 52 or 104-week window for each of four snapshot pairs, from manifests and a volume plan; a change-by-gap curve fitted over each family's settled warm weeks and tied to its replicate pairs; an adoption lag fitted on 42 family rollouts and carried week by week onto 24 planned releases. The work is long, not clever.
Layer 1: weekly score files re-sent after a late label load (the later load supersedes); merchant ids re-keyed at an acquirer migration, with a crosswalk; family codes in two schemes between the registry and the manifests; a manifest field renamed at the runtime rollout; weeks stamped by Monday date in one system and by ISO week in another; merchants that leave the book and stop being scored. Each is undone by reflex and none moves a source across a band line (`checks.py` asserts each one's effect on every graded figure).
Not one thing: the strongest single piece is S1 (the bootstrap pairs). A run that nails it and nothing else still leaves optimisation noise and feature releases on monitor, and earns 22 of 100.
One-fact test: the most useful single sentence is "a bootstrap pair resamples the whole training window, while two quarter-end models share every week of it but the thirteen that left and the thirteen that entered". It rescues R3 only, 1 of 8 results, and only once the per-row noise is taken net of the seeds and carried to each family's rows out and in, span by span.
Clean-data test: on perfectly cleaned files with every sentence read correctly, the reflex route (each source read off its own instrument, retunes and label revisions scaled by their counts, the calendar's dates for pinned views, merchants' own change as the remainder) is a reasonable analysis that closes its own budget and files label revisions and turnover in escalation and the other three on monitor, wrong on three sources.
Not semantics: every field, unit and clause read correctly (points, the bands, the snapshot calendar, what a replicate pair and a bootstrap pair are, the calendar's completed date as the day an image is released) still leaves each instrument's figure taken as its source's movement between snapshots.
Not rule application: the standard files what a source's movement is, how the book is weighted, the bands, the shares and the removal test, and no procedure. No document says how a source's movement at the snapshot gap is built from an instrument; applying the bands to any wrong estimate is what the hurried routes do.
Estimand: the stability standard, section 3: a source's movement is the root-mean-square change in a merchant's score between two consecutive quarter-end snapshots that the source alone produces, every scored merchant counted once and families weighted by their scored merchants at the latest snapshot, for an assessment year the root of the mean of its expected mean-square changes over the four snapshot pairs that close in that year (3.2); section 4: under 5.5 points monitor, 5.5 to under 8 a remediation plan, 8 or more escalation; section 5: a source's share is its mean-square movement over the sum of the model-side sources', and merchants' own change is the record's mean-square movement between snapshots over the pairs closed in the year the assessment is made, less the model-side sources over those pairs as they stood then, carried into the assessment year and reported as a share of that year's movement (own change and the sources' movements for that year together); section 6: the covenant test is repeated with every source placed in a plan or escalation taken out in full; section 2: the assessment year's release load is the feature roadmap's and its training volumes the data plan's, as filed. The covenant schedule files the snapshot calendar (every thirteenth weekly refresh), the tier cut-offs and the cap.
No decoy: no memo, draft or dashboard figure prices a source at the snapshot gap or ranks the sources. Each instrument answers its own question correctly (what one refit's seed, one resample of the window, one retune, one release, one week's label revisions does to a score). The change calendar answers its own question (when an image is released).
No replay: no result re-derives a published figure; no stated number is wrong.
Pinned: each result's rivals are run in `core.py` (34 variants of the golden route; the lattice of seven shortcut routes; the direct and rigorous routes; the four run routes with 13 further settings; three over-corrections), every honest variant inside its band and every shortcut ruled out by named data in `## Semantic fork table`.
Run forecast: R3 method shortcut and self-confirming check (the bootstrap spread taken as turnover; the budget still closes); R4 method shortcut (the replicate variance at any gap, or thirteen refits added up); R5 dismissed own evidence (the manifests of past rollouts on file, the calendar's dates kept); R6 self-confirming check (the thirteen-week budget's closure taken as validation); R7 and R8 unfinished chain and dismissed own evidence carried through (the reflex route's shares, bands and tier changes).
Strategy label: own (a variance attribution where each instrument sees its source at another unit than the decision's)
Depth level: 5: three independent judgments on three axes (the unit a bootstrap resamples, what a replicate reruns, when a rollout is in use), the three single drops on three distinct wrong calls and the lattice's seven shortcut routes on seven.
Lesson: an instrument that reruns one step of a training pipeline answers a narrower question than "what does this source move a score between two decision dates", and the answer is built by carrying each instrument to the decision's gap through the record that shows how the step repeats (the rows that turn over, the noise that is inherited, the fleet that adopts). No build on file teaches the variance of a difference between overlapping fits, inherited training noise read from a change-by-gap curve, or a platform change dated by its fleet's use.
