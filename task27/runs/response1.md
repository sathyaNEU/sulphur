Assumptions and judgment calls.
The Charter defines bottlenecks and stage timers but never prescribes holdback hours. I therefore treat observed dwell as additive components and estimate them from matched operational cells rather than assume all delay in a flagged container belongs to one cause: discharge-date chamber utilization for DC hold (>=95.0%, jump isolated by threshold scan), unload-date portal rejection for depot hold, and baseline rail dwell for BIP transfer delay. This is the decisive modeling choice. The measured fixed-effect increments, 20.1032 h DC and 10.5503 h depot, are aggregate estimates, not per-container observed work-order quantities; residual within-cell variation remains. Round increments 20.0 h / 10.5 h move totals by under 0.03% and no designation.
A different, defensible but I judge less supported rule - assigning 100% of flagged containers' yard / dray stage excess to DC / depot rather than only the measured increment - would materially change the slate: reefer_plug falls to 2,474.36 (MONITOR), drayage_carrier to 1,823.75 (MONITOR), coldstore_dock rises to 4,311.15 and empty_depot to 5,526.64; rail_handoff would be 2,609.96 under the same BIP handling. That yields a four-cause slate (vessel_berth, coldstore_dock, empty_depot, rail_handoff), not the six-cause recommendation. I rejected it because matched cells show the DC / depot events add ~20.10 / ~10.55 h atop terminal / network dwell, not replace it; erasing those independent components would attribute congestion the DC / depot did not originate. If Charter governance intends whole-stage ownership rather than component attribution, the recommendation must be re-cut; under the additive reading supported by the data it would not.
DC utilization is measured at discharge date because that date gave the tightest fixed-effect separation (gate-ready / DC dates mixed holdback with later dwell); depot rejection is measured at unload-completion date at the destination depot in Europe/Berlin; rail baseline is the measured non-inspected non-roll elapsed handoff, not the 6-hour free time, so routine dwell is not charged to BIP. These change phyto / rail by 191.50 days but no threshold.
Threshold comparison uses unrounded Scheduled days; register displays two decimals. Q3 is discharge-date quarter; the planned-shuttle date extracted from IDs equals discharge date for all 15,723 rail rows, so the rail corridor exception changes nothing.
"Avoidable expenditure" is applied exactly as Charter Clause 4.1 writes it - 100% of designated exposure - and I do not represent it as realized savings, carrier liability, or settlement cash.
The review date 18 January 2027 and named governance owners are presentation context, not data findings; the Charter effective date is 18 November 2026.
