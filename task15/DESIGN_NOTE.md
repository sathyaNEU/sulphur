### The process behind the files

Written before any result was named (`stumping` 1.1). Each year Banyena sows a new intake of lines in Stage 1 at two
sites (two replicates; between 268 and 337 lines, a different number every year), advances about a quarter to Stage
2 the next year at five sites, and about twenty of those to Stage 3 at ten sites (three replicates), where some of a
year's Stage 3 lines are kept for a second Stage 3 season. Lines come out of crosses, several sister lines to a
cross, and sisters share part of what they are. Every trial carries four check varieties, Byaduk among them. Lines
are advanced at the end of each season on that season's trial means with some breeder's taste, and a line gets a WI
number when it reaches Stage 3. The harvest system records every plot: its trial, replicate, block and position,
the weight off the plot and its moisture, the share of the plot lodged, and whether the plot was harvested or
abandoned (badly lodged plots the harvester cannot pick up are left). Seven of the ten sites are dryland Mallee and
Wimmera sites, two sit on the dry edge of the high-rainfall zone and one (Kerang) is irrigated; the season's water
at a site is in the weather and irrigation records, not in the plot file. Each year the head of breeding nominates
three Stage 3 lines to the High Rainfall Barley Series on an index of her own: the line's mean percentage of Byaduk
at the three wetter sites over its Stage 3 seasons. The nomination quotes that index. The series then grows each
nominee for two seasons at ten high-rainfall sites beside Byaduk and other breeders' entries (its entry list
changes size every year, and now and then it loses a trial before harvest), harvests every plot, lodged or not, and
publishes each entry's site means and lodging. What nobody at Banyena sees directly: how much of a quoted index was
the luck that got the line picked; that Byaduk, a high-rainfall variety, gains on Banyena's dryland-bred lines with
every millimetre of season water, and on some lines far more than on others; what lodging costs a weak-strawed line
at sites lusher than any of Banyena's own; and that the lines of one sowing year rise and fall together in the
series, because Byaduk has good and bad seasons of its own.

## Data provenance

Source: generated by `task15/generator` from a simulated process (a staged barley breeding programme and the independent trial series its lines are nominated to); no outside dataset is used.
Behaves like the real thing: the generator simulates the programme year by year from 2014 to 2025 (lines drawn from crosses and cohorts, a different intake every year, trials laid out in replicates and incomplete blocks at ten sites whose seasons share good and bad years, plots harvested by a serpentine path, lodging scored and badly lodged plots abandoned, lines advanced on the season's means) and the series beside it (ten wetter sites, an entry list that changes every year, a trial lost now and then, every plot harvested, site means published), so the patterns below run through every file and no effect is placed for a result.
Genuine patterns: (1) trial yield rises and flattens with season water, with site and season scatter; (2) seasons are good or bad across all sites together; (3) the lines' yield level creeps up by cohort; (4) sister lines of a cross share part of their level, response and straw; (5) block effects and a slope along the range inside trials; (6) lodging grows with how lush the trial is and with a line's weak straw; (7) plot error grows with the trial's yield; (8) Byaduk has good and bad seasons of its own in the series; (9) harvest runs several days with the clock, lunch breaks and moisture drifting.
Problems by process: the harvest logger was replaced before the 2019 season (two layouts, two units, two status codings); the old logger writes its header at every trial; samples that read oddly are weighed again and both rows are kept; a plot too lodged to pick up has a lodging figure and no weight; a line changes code when it reaches Stage 3 and four seasons' lists were keyed differently; rain comes from the bureau's monthly files, with a gauge out for a month now and then, and Kerang's waterings from its own log; the series publishes site means, never plots, and nothing for a trial it lost.
Breadth: 18 files; 34,662 plots in 179 trials over twelve seasons, 3,597 lines of which 28 are candidates, 119 series trials with 3,511 published entry means; deciding which trials, lines and seasons bear on a nominee is work.
