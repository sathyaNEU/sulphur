## Gate G reconciliation
The design note's "Not one thing" section asserts three independent judgments (root-trace weighting, dropped-hours fill, incident-cause dating) each move the over-budget list and each alone leaves it wrong, with the largest single hint rescuing 0 of 6 results (effort.json, "measured"). The design note's own **Hidden factor record** classifies the "whole traces kept at the edge" factor as: found by "a targeted query," fix depth "a reweight by the root's keep share, routine once seen," and verdict "keep" — the "keep" rests entirely on the claim that it is "not only in the deciding units" (it moves all four internal services and every interval), not on any claim that it is hard to find.

That is exactly the gap the judge's finding exploits. Gate H3's shallow-hidden-factor test is an OR across "sits only in the deciding units" **or** "shows in one cross-tab or plot" — not an AND. The judge's recomputation shows the factor shows in exactly one groupby (`parent_span_id.isin(span_ids)` by service), which the design note's own table concedes ("found by: a targeted query"). Under a literal reading of H3, breadth of impact across four services does not rescue a factor that is this cheaply discoverable and this cheaply fixed; the note's "keep" verdict answers a question (does it decide only one service?) that H3 does not actually ask.

The design note's measured effort table (quick 0/6, careful-but-hurried 2.5/6 ≈ 42%, thorough 6/6) and its "largest single hint rescues 0 of 6" line are both calibrated against a prior run's recorded habits (own-hourly-count reweighting, export-hours-only reading), not against a run that does ordinary trace-observability hygiene first (check span-kind/parent completeness before trusting any rate derived from a trace export — exactly the move the client's own caution in CLAUDE.md names as something "the careful column earns"). The judge's independent effort estimate (quick ~1-2/8 ≈ 15-25%, careful-but-hurried ~5-6/8 ≈ 62-75%, thorough 8/8) puts the careful-but-hurried score much closer to the thorough score than the design intends, and its one-fact estimate ("four of ten services never appear as a trace root ... the trace file is also missing December's first 11 days which still count per clause 2.2" rescues ~5-6 of 8) is well above the design note's own "0 of 6" and above the client's one-third ceiling (H5).

On the incident-attribution side, the design note treats R2 (root-cause owner per incident) as one result among six, built from seven independently-constructed incidents. The judge's finding that INC-1611 and INC-1619 — the two incidents the design note itself calls the hardest ("the largest-than-the-fault form... decides fares and notifications," architecture 7 stage-2 notes) — share the identical shape (naive culprit = first-named service; committed answer = a later-added service via earliest-changepoint) is not addressed anywhere in the design note's Gate G discussion. The note frames the onset-dating method as one modelling result (R2, "investigation loops"), but does not test whether the *same* onset-dating insight, once learned on one incident, transfers mechanically to the other. That is a legitimate open question the note never closes, and it pushes `surface_read_dependency` on R2 toward yes for at least the two hardest asks.

## Stump power (Gate F)
The judge's assessment: the sampling-correction insight is large and real (not a coin-flip trap), but it is visible on sight via standard trace-observability hygiene (one groupby plus one join to the LB file), so a careful-but-hurried frontier run plausibly finds the zero-root-span split and the keep-share gradient without needing to be "caught out" by it. The CI construction under the clustered/imputed design and the incident-minutes reconciliation against the status page remain genuinely narrower, separately-earnable pieces of work, but they carry less of the graded total than the design intends. Not too-easy overall, but the task's actual difficulty is more concentrated in one mechanism than "difficulty throughout" requires.
