Assumptions and judgment calls.

* Transfer attribution is material. Neighbor increases are treated as non-provider redistribution, supported by connected topology and matching demand patterns. Switching logs and provider asset-meter data were not supplied. Crediting all own-primary event relief would put Hartwell 0.43 MW below firm capacity, changing its classification. The guideline excludes other measures, so apparent transfer relief is not credited.
* Larger awards require extrapolation. Every future award exceeds the historical maximum scheduled volume. The calculation assumes proportional provider effectiveness at those larger volumes; the data do not directly validate that scaling.
* Cold-day baselines require extrapolation. The current-winter unscheduled samples do not reach -3.0 C. Linear fits are supported by validation and sensitivity checks, but dispatch is not randomized and model adjustment cannot eliminate all attribution uncertainty.
* The two target half-hours are averaged, not summed. Separate estimates for interval ends 17:30 and 18:00 retain the same decision. Partial-weather means and the treatment of cancellations were also tested.
* Delivery timing needs resolution. The guideline instructs commitment in December 2026 for winter 2027/28, but its stated 18-month lead time reaches June 2028. The files flag this inconsistency; the recommendation is to commit the two reinforcements, not to claim that winter-start energisation has been demonstrated.

Alternatives rejected.

* Credit the contracted awards at face value: this incorrectly clears all four constraints. Estimated provider delivery is materially below 1 MW per MW scheduled.
* Credit every reduction seen at the contracted primary: this includes apparent transfer relief and incorrectly clears Hartwell. The workbook’s Provider Evidence and Sensitivity sheets show the distinction.
* Ignore flexibility entirely: this flags all four primaries, disregarding demonstrated provider reductions and the requirement to count winter contracts.
* Use asset health, interruption performance or tariffs to choose the capacity reinforcements: those inputs address different questions and do not override the supplied demand-versus-firm-capacity rule.
