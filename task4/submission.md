## Critical Components

1. The pilot ran the thrifty blocks: the nine blocks the six electric buses ran on weekdays average **46.2** litres of diesel per 100 km against **49.1** for the other 39 (a gap of **2.9** l/100 km, graded within 1.0); among the 33 weekday blocks of 225 km or less on the 2024 timetable (the pilot buses' range) they sit in the thriftiest half, and none of the nine is among the ten thriftiest of the 48 or among its hardest sixteen. Kilometres, climb and timetable speed do not carry that gap (the bridge from diesel use to the pilot's energy per km, fitted over the 49 blocks an electric bus ran with their diesel figures, has a slope of 0.0309 kWh per km per l/100 km and a residual of 0.024 kWh per km), so a fit carried on those covariates forecasts the hard blocks light; the diesel figures carry every block's difficulty to the design day.

2. The pilot buses' fuel-fired cabin heaters supplied about **4.3 kW** of heat on a -15 C day (graded within the band 3.9 to 4.7 kW): 0.48 litres of HVO an hour, from the electric buses' HVO fills on the fuel-card statement tank to tank against the days' hours and temperatures, at the heater's rating of 35 kW for 3.9 l/h. The 2027 model heats electrically with resistive boosters below -10 C, so on the design day it draws that heat from the battery on top of the 8.1 kW the pilot's HVAC drew: 12.4 kW of heating an hour out, 40 to 60 kWh a block.

3. On the design night every plugged-in bus takes about **3.5 kW** through its charger beside the recharge (graded within the band 2.8 to 4.2 kW, which every reading of the night's temperature and of the cold's knot spans), for as long as it is on charge between 21:00 and 07:00: each session's metered energy splits into the battery's gain (its state-of-charge points times the capacity its own day shows) over the chargers' efficiency and a steady draw over the session's hours on charge inside the night period that rises as the night's mean falls below a few degrees (the battery held at its charging temperature). The sessions' pooled ratio of gain to metered energy, 0.920, averages that draw over a year of mostly mild nights; at -15 C it understates each qualifying bus's night by about 20 kWh and puts 18 buses through the night where 17 fit.

## Step-by-Step

1. Clean and join the operating records: the dispatch log's block codes before 11 August 2025 to the register's current codes and its early-2024 registrations to fleet numbers; the fuel-card statement's cards to vehicles by the register's validity dates, the night shift's spare cards by the fleet number typed at the pump; the telematics days the portal uploaded twice dropped; blank ambient readings filled from the airport station (the town station less its 0.90 C offset where the airport has its April 2023 gap); the charging sessions' UTC stamps put on local time (summer time included) and each session given its evening.

2. Diesel use per block (ask 1): for each diesel bus, consecutive diesel fills on the statement with a second swipe within twelve minutes merged into one fill; the litres of each fill are the fuel burnt on the bus-days the dispatch log shows between that fill and the one before, by departure time; over November 2025 to October 2026 the litres of every interval are regressed on the scheduled kilometres each block contributed to it, jointly over every block the intervals touch (the weekend blocks included, since a bus's fills span both), giving one rate per block (37.2 to 62.4 l/100 km). Charging each fill to the block the bus ran on the day of the fill, or giving every block its commonest bus's yearly rate, puts most blocks several litres off because the buses rotate over the blocks.

3. The pilot's record: the nine weekday blocks with 30 or more electric bus-days sit in the middle of the 48 by diesel use (none among the ten thriftiest, none among the hardest sixteen) and in the thriftiest half of the 33 blocks of 225 km or less on the 2024 timetable (the planner picked the blocks from the fuel figures and the buses' range), averaging 46.2 against 49.1 l/100 km for the rest (component 1). Beside them the electric buses ran three short stand-in blocks and the weekend blocks, which give the bridge its reach over harder blocks.

4. The pilot's energy model: on each electric bus-day the energy outside heating and cooling (energy used less HVAC) per kilometre, by block, weighted by kilometres; the HVAC draw per hour out in 1 C bins of the day's ambient. The HVAC draw flattens at about 8.1 kW below -10 C on the pilot buses, because the vehicle register's fuel-fired heater (35 kW, 3.9 l/h) carries the rest of the cabin's heat below about -2 C: the electric buses' HVO fills on the statement, tank to tank over the days between, fit 0.48 l/h at -15 C, 4.3 kW of heat (component 2; the two buses whose heater the workshop log shows out in early 2026 drew the extra heat from the battery, 8.0 and 7.1 kWh per litre against the rating's 8.97). The 2027 model has no fuel heater and runs its boosters below -10 C, so its heating on the design day is 12.4 kW an hour out.

5. The bridge and the energies (ask 2): the per-km energy of the 49 blocks an electric bus ran regressed on their diesel rates (a = -0.1328, b = 0.03092, residual 0.024 kWh per km); for each weekday block the design-day energy is its scheduled km times the bridged per-km energy (the block's own electric figure where it has 30 or more electric days) plus its hours out times 12.4 kW. Adding the hours per km to the bridge, or bridging every block, moves no energy by more than one 10 kWh bin. Carrying the pilot's fit on kilometres, climb and timetable speed instead puts the hard blocks light and lets 29 blocks qualify.

6. The limit (asks 3 and 4): each electric bus-day's energy over its state-of-charge drop is the capacity the battery system shows; on age in service with a cold term and a level per bus, a form with a fast early loss and a steady loss after it (curved 4.33, square root 4.62, log 4.91 per cent; a straight line in age, which runs under the first months' fast loss, 2.99) gives 4.3 per cent in the first twelve months, 459 kWh usable on the 2027 model and a limit of 390 kWh; 23 blocks pass it, and every one of those forms, and the quoted 480 kWh itself, leaves every verdict where it is.

7. The charging split (component 3): on every session with a state-of-charge rise of 30 points or more, the charger's metered energy regressed on the battery's gain and on the hours plugged in inside the night period, the latter alone and times the night's cold below a knot profiled on half degrees (5.0 C), the night's temperature the mean of the evening's and the next morning's at the airport station; on this fit the efficiency is 0.948 and the draw 0.45 kW plus 0.150 kW a degree, 3.5 kW at -15 C. Taking the evening's or the morning's temperature alone, the bus's own sensor, or the knot at 0 or 10 C keeps the draw inside its band and the order at 17; a loss that scales with the recharge instead of the hours fits the sessions worse and lands the same order.

8. The night (ask 5 and the order): each qualifying block's bus is on charge from its return to its departure inside the night period, taking its recharge at up to 150 kW less its own steady draw and 3.5 kW beside it throughout; the 23 qualifying blocks need 9.1 MWh at the connection point, against at most 6.90 MWh the night period's 690 kW gives. Taken in the fleet plan's priority order, the list is cut at M145, the 18th, which the night cannot recharge together with the 17 above it under any schedule (the night's maximum flow over the blocks' windows falls short of it; a least-slack schedule recharges the 17). The six pilot buses go to Kauppala with the 2027 timetable (the fleet plan), so they draw nothing at Niittylä. Summing the per-block verdicts orders 23; the night at the sessions' pooled ratio orders 18; on battery energy alone 19. With the quoted capacity in place of the first winter's usable no block changes side, so the loss is reported for the limit it sets and moves no verdict.

9. Why no other conclusion survives: the pilot's fit carried on the timetable's covariates with the night at the pooled ratio orders 18 (29 without the night's supply) and the pilot's cold-day consumption times kilometres orders 33; on that pass the bridge put right alone orders 18 and the battery's conditioning put right alone 17, a different set; leaving out only the heaters' share orders 19, and the pilot's record carried as it stands 21. Only the bridge, the heaters' share and the conditioning together, with the first winter's loss and the night's capacity, land 17.
