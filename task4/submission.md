## Critical Components

1. The pilot ran the thrifty blocks: the nine blocks the six electric buses ran on weekdays average **46.2** litres of diesel per 100 km against **49.1** for the other 39 (a gap of **2.9** l/100 km, graded within 1.0); among the 33 weekday blocks of 225 km or less on the 2024 timetable (the pilot buses' range) they sit in the thriftiest half, and none of the nine is among the ten thriftiest of the 48 or among its hardest sixteen. Kilometres, climb and timetable speed do not carry that gap (the bridge from diesel use to the pilot's energy per km, fitted over the 49 blocks an electric bus ran with their diesel figures, has a slope of 0.0309 kWh per km per l/100 km and a residual of 0.024 kWh per km), so a fit carried on those covariates forecasts the hard blocks light; the diesel figures carry every block's difficulty to the design day.

2. The pilot buses' fuel-fired cabin heaters supplied about **4.3 kW** of heat on a -15 C day (graded within the band 3.9 to 4.7 kW): 0.48 litres of HVO an hour, from the electric buses' HVO fills on the fuel-card statement tank to tank against the days' hours and temperatures, at the heater's rating of 35 kW for 3.9 l/h. The 2027 model heats electrically with resistive boosters below -10 C, so on the design day it draws that heat from the battery on top of the 8.1 kW the pilot's HVAC drew: 12.4 kW of heating an hour out, 40 to 60 kWh a block.

## Step-by-Step

1. Clean and join the operating records: the dispatch log's block codes before 11 August 2025 to the register's current codes and its early-2024 registrations to fleet numbers; the fuel-card statement's cards to vehicles by the register's validity dates, the night shift's spare cards by the fleet number typed at the pump; the telematics days the portal uploaded twice dropped; blank ambient readings filled from the airport station (the town station less its 0.90 C offset where the airport has its April 2023 gap); the charging sessions' UTC stamps read against the local timetable.

2. Diesel use per block (ask 1): for each diesel bus, consecutive diesel fills on the statement with a second swipe within twelve minutes merged into one fill; the litres of each fill are the fuel burnt on the bus-days the dispatch log shows between that fill and the one before, by departure time; over November 2025 to October 2026 the litres of every interval are regressed on the scheduled kilometres each block contributed to it, giving one rate per block (37.2 to 62.4 l/100 km). Charging each fill to the block the bus ran on the day of the fill, or giving every block its commonest bus's yearly rate, puts most blocks several litres off because the buses rotate over the blocks.

3. The pilot's record: the nine weekday blocks with 30 or more electric bus-days sit in the middle of the 48 by diesel use (none among the ten thriftiest, none among the hardest sixteen) and in the thriftiest half of the 33 blocks of 225 km or less on the 2024 timetable (the planner picked the blocks from the fuel figures and the buses' range), averaging 46.2 against 49.1 l/100 km for the rest (component 1). Beside them the electric buses ran three short stand-in blocks and the weekend blocks, which give the bridge its reach over harder blocks.

4. The pilot's energy model: on each electric bus-day the energy outside heating and cooling (energy used less HVAC) per kilometre, by block, weighted by kilometres; the HVAC draw per hour out in 1 C bins of the day's ambient. The HVAC draw flattens at about 8.1 kW below -10 C on the pilot buses, because the vehicle register's fuel-fired heater (35 kW, 3.9 l/h) carries the rest of the cabin's heat below about -2 C: the electric buses' HVO fills on the statement, tank to tank over the days between, fit 0.48 l/h at -15 C, 4.3 kW of heat (component 2; the two buses whose heater the workshop log shows out in early 2026 drew the extra heat from the battery, 8.0 and 7.1 kWh per litre against the rating's 8.97). The 2027 model has no fuel heater and runs its boosters below -10 C, so its heating on the design day is 12.4 kW an hour out.

5. The bridge and the energies (ask 2): the per-km energy of the 49 blocks an electric bus ran regressed on their diesel rates (a = -0.1328, b = 0.03092, residual 0.024 kWh per km); for each weekday block the design-day energy is its scheduled km times the bridged per-km energy (the block's own electric figure where it has 30 or more electric days) plus its hours out times 12.4 kW. Adding the hours per km to the bridge, or bridging every block, moves no energy by more than one 10 kWh bin. Carrying the pilot's fit on kilometres, climb and timetable speed, with the pilot's own cold response read at -15 C, puts the hard blocks 60 kWh light on average and orders 21.

6. The limit (asks 3 and 4): each electric bus-day's energy over its state-of-charge drop is the capacity the battery system shows; on age in service with a cold term and a level per bus, a form with a fast early loss and a steady loss after it (curved 4.28, square root 4.57, log 4.86 per cent; a straight line in age 2.96) gives 4.3 per cent in the first twelve months, 460 kWh usable on the 2027 model and a limit of 391 kWh; 23 blocks pass it.

7. The night (ask 5 and the order): the 23 qualifying blocks need 8.7 MWh at the connection point (their energies over the charging efficiency the sessions show, 0.92), against at most 6.90 MWh the night period's 690 kW can deliver between 21:00 and 07:00. Taken in the fleet plan's priority order, each between its return and its departure inside the night period at up to 150 kW a bus and 690 kW in all, the list is cut at M124, the 19th, which the night cannot recharge together with the 18 above it under any schedule (the night's maximum flow over the blocks' windows falls short of it; a least-slack schedule recharges the 18). The six pilot buses enter neither figure: DES-1 4.3 orders one bus for each block assigned and 4.2 tests the night on the assigned blocks, so whatever they do after the new buses arrive moves neither the count nor the night. Summing the per-block verdicts orders 23; scheduling on battery energy without the charging losses orders 19. With the quoted capacity in place of the first winter's usable no block changes side, so the loss is reported for the limit it sets and moves no verdict.

8. Why no other conclusion survives: the pilot's own fit carried on the timetable's covariates and the pilot's cold response orders 21 (38 without the night's supply) and the pilot's cold-day consumption times kilometres orders 33; putting the diesel bridge right alone orders 20 and the heaters' share right alone orders 18; only both together, with the first winter's loss, the charging losses and the night's capacity, land 18.
