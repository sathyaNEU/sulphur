## Deep reasoning

Task type: regression-classification-model-diagnostics (forecasting a returnable container fleet's shortfall from fitted stock relations and their diagnostics, house inventory and demand planning), which serves Forecasting.
Domain test: every result is a quantity returnable-asset planning estimates: a pipeline stock by holder from sampled counts of part of the holding (full containers at one point of the delivery cycle, the empties rebuilt from the line's own dockets), a cycle stock from run sizes and the range on sale, an attrition rate from the age structure of the lots a count finds against a register that never writes off, and a peak-week shortfall against a float standard with its weather range.
Swap: "How many rental cars of each class will we be short in our worst week next summer?" keeps the banded cover and the forecast and nothing else: a rental car is on a contract or on the lot and both are recorded, no count sees part of what a holder has, nothing arrives a production run at a time, and a lost car is written off. The task stops making sense.
Where the weight sits: R2 to R4, and R2 and R3 most. R2 turns 5,621 checks of full containers on the cut pack into what each line held on the day (each check joined to the day the dray last called at the account and to the line's weekly sales, its share of its type's deliveries over the record times the type's deliveries that week), fits what a line holds by pool and delivery interval, checks the rebuild on the counts' own fall, and carries the relation to every line, to the estate's cellars and to each summer week. R3 fits the share of each of some fifteen purchase lots the count found on site against the lot's age, for the yearly rate of leaving and the share of the fleet on site together, and turns the rate into the containers lost over the year to the count and per container delivered. R4 separates the store's stock into half a run per beer on sale and cover, then rebuilds it under next summer's runs and range.
Layer 1: delivery dockets keyed by drivers (blanks, a handheld re-sync that duplicates visits); cellar counts on paper forms keyed late, a rep's round missed in holiday weeks, a line counted twice on one call; outlet accounts renumbered on a change of owner; container codes written three ways (50, 50L and K50; firkins as FIR, 9G and F9); register lots with and without the maker's prefix, a lot keyed under its invoice year and its delivery year; count sheets by site with the purchase year read from the neck ring. None decides a result: every route cleans them the same way, and each one left uncleaned keeps every graded figure in its band and every pool's cover where it is (`checks.py` at the build).
Not one thing: the strongest single piece is the rebuild of what a line holds. A run that nails it still files the 50 litre pool as a seasonal hire (1,279 on the cut pack against a minimum run of 3,500), the 30 litre pool as a hire (564), the 20 litre pool with kegs to spare (-641) and the firkins with casks to spare (-71), four covers wrong, because the store and the loss are untouched (S1 fixed alone on the reflex route earns 16 on the rubric).
One-fact test: "a rep's cellar count is of the full containers of a line on its order day, and the empties wait there for the dray" rescues R2 and nothing else (1 of 7 results), and leaves R2's own rebuild to do: the store, the loss and every figure downstream still fall with S2 and S3.
Not semantics: every field, unit, code and clause read correctly leaves the reflex route where it is. The count's column is full containers and is read as full containers; the register is the list of containers bought and not condemned and is read as that; the standard's requirement, fleet, season and lines are as written. The route still files four covers wrong because no file holds the empties, the fleet alive or next summer's store, and each has to be estimated.
Not rule application: the fleet standard files what a requirement, a fleet, a shortfall, the season, the two cases and the lines are; it performs none of R2 to R6. After every filed rule is applied the analyst still has to estimate what the trade holds in a summer week, what the store holds under the 2027 plan and what the trade loses.
Estimand: the fleet standard FS-04, clauses 2.1 (a pool's requirement in a week: its containers at customers, full, on dispense or empty and awaiting collection, its containers full in the cold store, at the depots, in wash and repair, and a float of two days' deliveries), 2.2 (a pool's fleet in a week: its containers on Wrenfold's sites or at its customers), 2.3 (the shortfall: requirement less fleet with no purchase or hire, a negative figure a surplus), 3.1 (the figure reviewed: the pool's largest weekly shortfall in the summer season, the weeks beginning Monday 3 May to Monday 20 September 2027), 3.2 (the planning case: every week at the weather station's normal for the week), 3.3 (the hot case: the season run on the recorded weather of each of the ten summers 2017 to 2026, the second highest of the ten largest weekly shortfalls), 4.1 (no shortfall in the planning case: no cover; a shortfall under the maker's minimum run: a seasonal hire; the minimum run or more: a purchase; the hot case is reported beside it), 5.1 (empty containers are collected by the dray at its next delivery to the account); the maker's quotation (the minimum run per container type); the Maplestead supply agreement's schedule (the lines by container type, their listing dates and weekly volumes); the 2027 brew plan (each beer's container types, racking run and on-sale dates).
No decoy: no memo, draft or column carries a wrong answer; no document argues for a route; no file ranks or sizes the pools on the question. The register, the count sheet, the cellar counts and the brew plan are what they say they are.
No replay: no result re-derives a published figure and no filed number is wrong.
Pinned: twenty variants of the golden's conventions in `generator/core.py` at the draw (listed under ## Pinning list; the build's battery in `generator/analysis.py` takes each convention alone and every two together) file one cover for every pool and set every band; each fitted input moved 20 per cent alone (the season's deliveries 5), and all four moved two measured spreads to one side together, file the same cover (`core.py --forcing`); the readings refused are each refused by a figure (## Semantic fork table).
Run forecast: R2 lost to dismissed own evidence (the form shows the count is of full containers, the run notes it and fits the counts as they stand, or adds an allowance it does not measure) or to a method shortcut (the counts filtered to the calls soonest after a delivery in place of the rebuild); R3 lost to an unfinished chain (the lots' thinning seen in the count sheet and no rate estimated, the fleet carried forward with condemnations only) or to a method shortcut (every absence from site read as loss); R4 lost to a self-confirming check (the store's weeks of cover hold on a held-out 2026, because the runs and the range did not change in the record); R5 to R7 fall with whichever of the three a run loses. The strongest run is expected to make one of the three and lose two.
Strategy label: other (each part of an unrecorded stock assigned to the record that sees it; no label in `references/sulfur-strategies.md` names a count that sees part of a holding, a run-sized store stock and attrition read from an age structure together)
Depth level: 5: three independent judgments, each necessary and none sufficient, each two-sided, the three single drops on three different wrong sets of covers and the seven shortcut routes of the lattice on four.
Lesson: a stock nobody counts is still pinned by the records that see part of it: the trade's by a count of its full containers and the dockets that say how long each line has been drawing, the missing containers by how the lots thin with age on site, the store's by the runs it receives and the beers on sale; and each record read as if it were the whole (the count as the holding, the register as the fleet, this year's cover as next year's) understates the need. No build on file teaches a holding rebuilt from a unit's own delivery cycle, or attrition estimated from the age structure of survivors against a register that never writes off.
