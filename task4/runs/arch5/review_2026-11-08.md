Feedback

What's good:
This is a well built Project Sulfur task. The prompt asks a real fleet question (how many battery electric tractors to order for the Varnamo duties on the 2027 shortlist, and which duties they take over under FES-2), and the shipped data package supports it properly: the Jonkoping pilot telematics in bev_daily_summary_2025-03_2026-10.csv, the declared weights in tms_consignments_jonkoping_2025-2026.csv, the Varnamo weighings in weighbridge_varnamo_2024-11_2026-10.csv, the legs and climbs in route_register_2026.xlsx, the cutoff in fleet_electrification_standard_FES-2.pdf, the usable battery in quotation_bev_tractors_2027.pdf and the shortlist in fleet_plan_2027.xlsx. The messy data work is genuine (a day uploaded twice, declared weights in kilograms or in tonnes with a decimal comma, ambient dropouts filled from daily_mean_temperature_2016-2026.csv, the last weighing of each CMR).

The committed answer holds up. It reproduces from the shipped data: calibrating each pilot shipper's declared weights on the Varnamo weighbridge gives a payload term of 2.2 kWh per tonne per 100 km against 1.0 on the raw declarations, V14 comes out near 449 kWh and V03, V07 and V11 near 470 kWh against the 459 kWh limit (85 per cent of the 540 kWh usable battery, FES-2 4.3), and the order is 6 tractors (V02, V05, V09, V14, V16, V18). It is also the only answer the data supports: the Tabergs Trävaror days, whose declarations equal the weighbridge, give 2.19 on their own, which rules out the declared-weight reading, and pooling the calibration into one line (1.05) is correctly shown to hide the shipper differences. Domain fit is strong (measurement error correcting an attenuated regression slope in an energy forecast) and the deciding step is a real analytical one. Determinism, correctness and depth all pass.

What's bad:
The task is being returned only because it is not difficult enough: the test responses reached the committed answer too often. It is not being returned for correctness or determinism.

The reason is that the whole decision rests on one recognition, and the shipped data makes that recognition easy to confirm once a solver looks. The declared weights and the weighbridge sit side by side, FES-2 4.2 defines the planning load as the mean weighed net payload, so a careful solver naturally asks whether the pilot payloads are on the same basis. Once they compare declared against weighed loads shipper by shipper, the Tabergs days hand them a clean check (slope 1.000 on the weighbridge, 2.19 kWh per tonne per 100 km on their own) that confirms the corrected coefficient without having to trust the calibration method at all. One test response put it exactly that way: "The measured-weight evidence and independent TTV estimate support correcting this attenuation." The test response that missed it did not do a reasonable analysis that stopped short; it simply never checked the declarations, stating that it "used TMS declared payload for the pilot because no pilot weighbridge measurements were supplied."

After the calibration, nothing else moves the answer: every temperature form you could reasonably fit (the two cold rates at 16 C and 0 C, one rate at 15 C, quadratic, linear) still gives 6 tractors, and rounding, the design temperature and the usable battery are all pinned. So the cold-weather modelling, which is good work, never decides anything, and the task reduces to a single step that a strong solver either takes or skips.

What needs fixing:
- The difficulty rests on one step (spotting and correcting the noisy declared pilot weights); a second analytical step needs to co-decide the order.
- The Tabergs Trävaror exact declarations act as a built-in answer check for the corrected payload term (2.19 against 2.20), so the hard part can be confirmed without doing it.
- The cold-weather model choice never flips a duty: under calibration V03, V07 and V11 stay above 459 kWh under every temperature form, even linear (about 459.3 to 459.9).
- No figure or prose correction is needed in the committed answer; keep the 2.2 against 1.0 comparison and the 8-tractor declared-weight alternative in the memo.

How to fix it:
Keep the data package, the FES-2 rules and the determinism you already have, and make a second analytical step decide the order alongside the calibration. The natural candidate is already in your data: the cold curvature. Place one or two shortlisted duties (for example V14 or one of V03, V07, V11) so that, after correct calibration, a single rate below 16 C puts them on one side of 459 kWh and the two-rate model (below 16 C and below 0 C) on the other. Your own step 6 already shows the out-of-sample evidence that pins this (the single-rate model fitted without January and February 2026 under-predicts their sub-zero days by 3.4 per cent, the two-rate model by -0.4 per cent), so the estimand stays the design-day energy at -5 C and the answer stays unique, but a solver now has to get both the measurement error and the out-of-sample model choice right.

Then weaken the free confirmation: either drop Tabergs Trävaror from the pilot shippers or make its exact-declaration days too few to estimate the payload term on their own, so the corrected 2.2 has to come from shipper-specific regression calibration rather than from one clean subset. Check after each change that pooled calibration and declared weights still give a different order, and that the committed figures reproduce to the nearest 10 kWh.

Ways to make the task harder:
1. Make the temperature model co-decide: move the planning load or climb of one heavy duty so that, with calibrated payloads, the single cold rate keeps it under 459 kWh while the two-rate model, validated on held-out January and February 2026, pushes it over. A solver who calibrates but fits the simpler temperature curve then orders the wrong number.
2. Remove the clean control: take Tabergs Trävaror out of the pilot or cut its days sharply, so no subset of pilot days reproduces 2.2 directly and the attenuation correction has to be estimated and trusted on its own.
3. Make the calibration need more care per shipper: give one pilot shipper's declarations a different error pattern in the weighbridge file (for example scatter that grows with load, or a change partway through the window), so the expected weighed load given the declaration has to be modelled per shipper over the right period, while keeping it fully documented in the shipped data.
4. Tie the order to uncertainty in the forecast: if FES-2 required each duty to fit within 459 kWh with a stated margin for forecast error, stated plainly in the PDF, the solver would have to carry the extra variance from the calibrated payload into the design-day prediction, and a point-forecast-only answer would admit a duty that should stay diesel.
