Assumptions and judgment calls
Final hourly SCADA peak is used as the observable estimator of the peak 15-minute ISP demand. No in-scope site has a 15-minute station series; PQ covers only excluded modernized stations and registers are non-coincident monthly feeder maxima. This is a floor, not a true 15-minute measurement (max 15-minute demand >= hourly mean peak). It does not affect the filed numbers internally, but true ISP telemetry could re-tier sites; sensitivity below quantifies the first moves.
Peak-hour reactive demand is estimated from January settlement-meter net energy ratio times peak P, signed (negative = net capacitive), because no hourly site Q exists. This assumes January's net compensation state applies at the peak hour. A generic class-PF estimate would instead raise Kvernholm to 91.65% / 2.115 MVA Phase 2 and Hestmark to 113.36% / 10.068 MVA; I did not use it because settlement meters are actual site evidence and AMI Q ratios contradict the manual. A different reasonable Q choice can therefore change Kvernholm's tier, though not the two Phase 1 sites.
Legacy (non-AMI) phases are estimated, not observed, and GIS phase labels are rejected because they failed validation 75/220 against fitted AMI, while the fit matched 80/80 commissioning traces. Primary distributes legacy load by measured AMI same-site/class/phase peak shares. This is the largest judgment call; the GIS alternative changes the answer (below). Peak lateral sizing uses annual billed MWh because it correlates with AMI peak kW better than service points within site/class.
wmax uses active-power phase loads per Section 3.2, then multiplies total apparent S; it is not an apparent/reactive-weighted phase share.
Lower thresholds inclusive as Section 4.1 states; all filing values treated as estimates, not exact observed ISP truth; deficiency is disclosed rather than hidden.
Peak timestamps are reported in Oslo and UTC; the standard's calendar-year window is Oslo.
