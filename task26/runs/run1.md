3. Step-by-step process
Read vesterdal_planning_standard_ns2026.pdf first. It is a one-page VN-NS-2026-04 standard effective 18 March 2026. Sections 1.1-1.2 fix calendar-year 2026 peaks and independent site assessment with no portfolio cap; Section 2.1-2.2 names the four modernized exclusions and seven pool sites; Section 3.1-3.3 defines P_15m, Q_h, phase loading, S_15m = sqrt(P_15m^2 + Q_h^2), Lim_MVA = 3 x w_max x S_15m, Firm N-1 Loading Percentage = 100 x Lim_MVA / Firm_N1_MVA, Relief_MVA = max(0, Lim_MVA - 0.85 x Firm_N1_MVA); Section 4 fixes inclusive tiers; Section 5.1 fixes filing fields and pool roll-ups.

Read substation_transformer_register.xlsx, both sheets. Substations had 11 rows: four modernized 2023-2024 sites (Fossheim 54.0 MVA, Lindberg 62.0, Ostervik 48.0, Solberg 50.0) and the seven standards-bay pool sites with Firm N-1 ratings 45.4 (Drangvik), 34.0 (Hestmark), 31.2 (Kvernholm), 42.6 (Malmnes), 44.5 (Raudfjord), 33.6 (Svarthaug), 40.5 (Tverrli), plus R/X/tap metadata not needed by Section 3. Feeders had 70 feeder records and class/length used only for peak-class diagnostics.

Read and reconciled scada_substation_hourly_2026.csv: 101,160 data rows, 11 substations x 8,760 final hours = 96,360 in batch B20270112_FINAL plus 4,800 preliminary rows in B20270104_PRELIM covering January 1-20 for ten sites. I filtered to B20270112_FINAL and discarded 4,800 preliminary records after checking that preliminary active power averaged 98.500000% of final, 1.522843% low. Raudfjord alone used timestamp_local in 16.01.2026 19:00 with scada_clock CET_Standard; I parsed it as fixed UTC+01:00, yielding 18:00Z for its peak, rather than Europe/Oslo daylight rules. All other sites used the recorded Europe/Oslo timestamps and converted to UTC for lateral alignment. All peak timestamps fell on 16 January 2026. No peak P value was missing, blank, negative or duplicated at site-hour grain. 56 v_lv values were blank, all in June-July and none at a peak; voltage was not used for capacity. Feeder SCADA sums reconciled to substation peak P at the same UTC hour within 0.000040 MW.

Checked peaks rather than accepting feeder demand registers. feeder_demand_registers_monthly_2026.csv has 840 rows (70 feeders x 12 months), max_15min_demand_mw and month-end resets but no peak timestamp, while feeder_id_crosswalk.csv has 70 mappings with 33 pending renames. Determined AMI coverage from ami_lateral_telemetry_2026.db: 220 laterals (20 per each of 11 substations), 960,960 hourly lateral records, 4,368 hours per lateral across January-March and October-December 2026. Inferred true AMI phase rather than trusting GIS. 4. Assumptions and judgment calls
P_15m was estimated by the 2026 coincident SCADA peak hour, the only pool-wide coincident peak evidence; no pool site has 15-minute interval metering for its 2026 peak. This is the largest judgment call and not an observed interval maximum. If true coincident 15-minute peaks are higher than the listed multiples above, tiers can change: specifically Kvernholm above 1.017120x moves to Phase 1, Malmnes above 1.067976x moves to Phase 2, and Drangvik above 1.148123x moves to Phase 2; the +10.000000% stress would change the recommendation to Kvernholm Phase 1 and Malmnes Phase 2 with 35.467009 MVA pool relief. I retained the coincident SCADA estimate rather than invent interval peaks or adopt non-pool commissioning data.
Peak-hour Q was estimated as peak P times January revenue-meter Q/P and held at the peak hour. This preserves metered power factor including leading vars but assumes January monthly PF applies at peak. Manual class PF would change Kvernholm to Phase 1 and raise pool relief to 34.187429 MVA; I used settlement metering because it reflects actual 2026 operation.
AMI true phase was inferred from telemetry voltage, and peak phase shares were held at the recorded peak hour only. Legacy non-AMI phase was taken from GIS because it is the only phase record for unmetered laterals; that is the least-observed input. Discarding it or reassigning legacy by AMI distribution would move Kvernholm to Defer at 84.102818% and cut pool relief to 12.886061 MVA, so Kvernholm and Svarthaug need field phase verification before capital commitment. No better defensible legacy source exists in the records.
Peak lateral MW for legacy was estimated from AMI site/class peak-to-billed ratios, not metered legacy demand. Different reasonable legacy load estimates change w_max modestly but could move Kvernholm across the 85-100 band; the phase-driven sensitivity above brackets that risk.
Relief MWA is capacity above the 85.000000% planning ceiling, not transformer nameplate addition, construction volune, or post-2029 need. Defer is a capital-cycle decision, not a statement that deferred sites cannot later breach the ceiling. Modernized sites were excluded from scheduling even though their records validated phase inference.
No portfolio cap, load growth, contingency beyond Firm N-1, or cost was applied, per Sections 1.2-1.3 scope and the absence of such inputs in the standard.
5. Alternatives rejected
Summing January feeder demand-register maxima as P_15m: rejected because those are reset monthly, untimestamped and non-coincident; sums exceed pool SCADA peak-hour demand by 37.084292% (Kvernholm, 1.370843x) to 69.362548% (Malmnes, 1.693625x), and pending-rename feeders require DMS crosswalk. That method would manufacture phantom peak demand rather than estimate Section 3.1 coincident peak.
Using PQ commissioning 15-minute peaks for modernized Fossheim/Lindberg/Solberg: rejected because none is a pool site, coverage is September-October/December commissioning windows, and it does not observe January 16; using it would out-of-scope substitute interval evidence that does not exist for the seven sites. The risk this leaves is real and disclosed: actual interval peaks could exceed SCADA peak-hour estimates, and Kvernholm/Malmnes/Drangvik are the re-tier candidates identified above.
Trusting GIS phase labels for AMI laterals: rejected by evidence; inferred phases matched all 80 independent commissioning traces while GIS agreed only 20.000000%-35.000000% at most standard sites. GIS retained only for legacy because no AMI telemetry exists there; phase-driven threshold sensitivity is disclosed rather than treated as certainty.
Engineering-manual class PF as Q_h: rejected as primary because revenue meters show actual settlement PF, including leading vars at Kvernholm/Tverrli and Malmnes at 0.902679. Manual PF would flip Kvernholm to Phase 1 at 109.738194% / 7.718316 MVA and raise pool relief to 34.187429 MVA; it is a sensitivity, not the metered 2026 basis.
Ignoring legacy laterals or treating lateral-only phase shares as w_max: rejected because Section 3.2 requires all single-phase loads plus one-third balanced load, and legacy is over half of Kvernholm/Svarthaug peak. Omitting them would understate limiting-phase loading; the AMI-distribution alternative yields 12.886061 MVA pool relief but discards the only legacy phase record.
Scheduling by aggregate pool loading (83.438922%, below 85.000000%): rejected because Section 1.2 and 4 mandate independent site tiers and no portfolio override; aggregate below ceiling coexists with Svarthaug at 125.117702% and Kvernholm above its ceiling.
Evidence against the call that does not change it: PQ interval records show substation 15-minute peaks can exceed hourly means, and January register sums are far above coincident peaks; neither provides a pool-site January coincident interval peak, so they do not re-tier the schedule under the stated estimate. Kvernholm is deliberately close to Phase 1 (1.682294 percentage points); if actual interval metering shows more than 1.017120x its SCADA peak-hour demand, it must move to Phase 1 before appropriation. Svarthaug and Hestmark remain Phase 1 under manual Q (132.828388% / 16.070338 MVA and 115.584631% / 10.398775 MVA) and under +10.000000% peak stress.
