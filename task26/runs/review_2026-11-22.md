# Reviewer Feedback (task26 v3, Returned 2026-11-22)

## What's good
This is a serious Project Sulfur package. The Vesterdal Nett relief question is a real planning decision, and the 19 shipped files give it the texture of a utility planning office: hourly substation SCADA with superseded preliminary extracts and Raudfjord's legacy CET stamps, feeder SCADA in kW behind a legacy ID crosswalk, monthly feeder demand registers, settled net kVARh in revenue_meter_monthly_2026.xlsx, per-phase 22 kV bus voltages, AMI lateral telemetry in a SQLite database, and only two sites (Fossheim and Lindberg) with true 15-minute metering in pq_commissioning_autumn2026.csv, and those only for September and October. The planning standard (vesterdal_planning_standard_ns2026.pdf) pins the loading formula, the Firm N-1 basis, the 100% and 85% phase lines and the relief formula cleanly, and the pool scope (seven sites, excluding Fossheim, Lindberg, Ostervik and Solberg) is unambiguous.

The intended difficulty is genuine analysis, not a planted trick. A solver has to estimate a 15-minute coincident peak that is not measured at any in-scope site, recover peak-hour reactive demand without a reactive archive, and split load by phase across laterals where half have no AMI. The PQ commissioning data do clearly show that the hourly SCADA peak understates the 15-minute peak (Fossheim's coincident 15-minute maximum sits 2.89 MW above the hourly maximum in September and 3.81 MW above it in October; Lindberg's sits 11.45 and 12.01 MW above). So the committed answer is right to reject the 195.43 MW hourly figure. Both test responses took exactly that shortcut ("Final hourly SCADA peak is used as the observable estimator of the peak 15-minute ISP demand ... This is a floor, not a true 15-minute measurement"), and the shipped data rule that reading out.

## What's bad
The task is being returned for determinism: the shipped data support more than one defensible 15-minute peak estimator, and the defensible estimators place Malmnes in different phases.

NS-2026 Section 3.1 defines the quantity ("annual 15-minute peak") but no shipped file says how to estimate it at a site without 15-minute metering. The committed answer uses one specific construction: hourly peak plus k_ord x sqrt(d'Rd), with R taken from feeder innovation correlations after stripping four SVD modes, and k_ord = 1.0454 calibrated on the autumn PQ data. That is one reasonable choice, not the only one the data allow.

A direct, simpler estimator fits the same evidence equally well. Malmnes is a near-exact statistical twin of Fossheim in the shipped feeder data: raw feeder correlation 0.578 at both, log-difference correlation 0.390 at both, profile-residual correlation 0.742 at both, January register excess 22.74 vs 21.12 MW, and hourly peak 30.57 vs 30.41 MW. Calibrating the 15-minute uplift on Fossheim's own measured PQ excess (ratio of measured excess to register excess, then applied to Malmnes's January register excess) predicts October from September with a mean absolute error of about 0.6 MW, the same as the 0.62 MW the committed answer reports for its own method. That estimator gives Malmnes a 15-minute active peak of 35.8 to 37.4 MW. With the committed answer's own reactive figure (-0.69 MVAr) and phase share (34.98%), Malmnes loads at 80.9% to 84.5% of Firm N-1, which is Defer. The committed answer has 41.24 MW, 93.29% and Phase 2. Pool relief falls from 54.97 MVA to about 51.1 MVA, and pool 15-minute demand to about 238 MW, outside the committed band of 240.1 to 244.3 MW. No shipped winter 15-minute measurement can separate a Malmnes winter excess of about 10.7 MW (committed) from about 6 MW (twin-calibrated). The same twin check on Drangvik against Lindberg gives 107% to 109%, which agrees with the committed Phase 1, so the problem is confined to the Malmnes tier and the pool totals.

The committed answer's own sensitivity work points the same way. Its band for Malmnes is 85.8% to 96.9%, right against the 85% line, and several of its own listed alternatives (independent quadrature, 1-PC stripping, pooled register coincidence, flat PQ ratio) move Malmnes or Drangvik across a phase line. Those alternatives are rejected only on the MAE table in Step 3, and that table could not be reproduced from the shipped files with the natural reading of d: with d as register maximum minus feeder hourly maximum and Fossheim's R near 0.07, sqrt(d'Rd) comes out near 6 MW against a measured 2.9 to 3.8 MW. Because the deciding comparison cannot be checked from the shipped data, it cannot rule out the competing estimators.

There is also a prose error. Step 8 says Malmnes's thinnest clearance is "93.29% vs 100.0%", but Malmnes's binding boundary in the committed answer is the 85% Phase 2 line.

## What needs fixing
- The 15-minute peak estimator for sites without 15-minute metering is not pinned in any shipped file, and a twin-calibrated estimator that fits the PQ data as well as the committed method moves Malmnes from Phase 2 (93.29%) to Defer (80.9% to 84.5%).
- Pool total relief (54.97 MVA) and pool 15-minute active demand (242.89 MW) depend on that unpinned choice; the alternative gives about 51.1 MVA and about 238 MW.
- The Step 3 MAE comparison (0.62 MW vs 3.22 to 9.32 MW for every alternative) does not reproduce from pq_commissioning_autumn2026.csv and the feeder registers with the obvious definition of the register excess d.
- Step 8 states Malmnes's clearance against 100.0%; it should be against the 85% Phase 2 line.
