## Final Recommendation

**Put Downtown, Lakeview, Old Town, Riverside and Sundquist into the 2027-2029 main replacement program, and send the 2027 meter changeout to Elm Grove, Halvorsen, Lakeview, North End, Prairie Rose, Riverside and Sundquist.** Their leak loss in the twelve months to September 2026 was 1.21 to 1.61 per cent of their gas in (Riverside 1.25, Lakeview 1.21, Sundquist 1.35). Reject the two-district list (Downtown and Old Town) that the 2024-25 logger study's straight line on the airport's air gives: in the deep-snow winter of 2025-26 the gas in the buried services stayed near the soil's temperature, so that line books 0.7 to 0.9 points of each outdoor district's gas as meter error that was in fact leaking. Reject also the lists that spread each read evenly over its days (it adds Halvorsen) and that book every collapse in use as diversion (it drops Lakeview, whose new snowbirds took nothing).

## Step-by-Step

1. Rebuilt each regulator station's faulty gas days from the city gate less the other stations and the gate's own small difference: Riverside's January 12 to 25 communication gap, Lakeview's December transducer failure (the corrector substituted 25 psig for 35) and Westgate's stuck temperature transmitter; Riverside's January gas in is 22,715.3 Mcf and the fourteen districts' twelve months 1,844,373.
2. Turned the AMR reads into actual-to-actual periods per meter (the June 2025 extract's repeated rows dropped, estimated reads folded into the next actual read, four-dial rollovers and multipliers, final and initial reads at each exchange) and spread each period's billed volume over its days in proportion to its district's daily regulator-station volume: College Hill's January 2026 is 16,638.8 Mcf, where spreading evenly over the days gives 15,647.2, because the student rentals sat nearly empty from mid-December to mid-January.
3. Corrected the meters without temperature compensation (the register's flag, or the model code where it is blank) to base conditions with the gas temperature the meter saw: outdoors the soil at 20 inches plus 0.181 of the air's difference from it and 1.7 F of the house wall's warmth when the air is below 45 F, fitted on the 2024-25 loggers and matched by the commercial correctors' own gas temperatures through 2025-26; indoors, near basement temperature. The logger study's straight line on the airport's air fitted its own winter, when air and soil fell together, but in 2025-26 the snow held the soil near freezing while the air fell as far, so the line puts Halvorsen's under-registration at 5.82 per cent against 4.61.
4. Took each account's monthly use against its own weather response in the year before: 61 collapsed for three months or more reaching into the heating season, outside a vacancy or an exchange (a student rental's summer away recurs every year); those whose summer base load also went (bypasses) or whose winter use lost its heating response but kept its base (furnace taps) took gas without authorization (15 accounts), and those whose winter use kept a reduced heating response and whose summer returned were away (Lakeview's new snowbirds). Booking every collapse as diversion counts 61 accounts and drops Lakeview's leak loss to 0.26 per cent.
5. Leak loss is gas in less the billed volume, the under-registration, the corrector customers, own use, the damage reports and the gas taken without authorization: Old Town 1.61; Riverside 1.25; College Hill 0.78; North End 0.59; Westgate 0.40; Lakeview 1.21; Prairie Rose 0.25; Heritage Park 0.18; East Industrial 0.59; Downtown 1.35; Airport 0.65; Elm Grove 0.69; Halvorsen 0.83; Sundquist 1.35. The careful but hurried route (the air line, reads spread evenly, every collapse booked) leaves only Downtown and Old Town over the line.
6. Rule 2 on the under-registration: Elm Grove, Halvorsen, Lakeview, North End, Prairie Rose, Riverside and Sundquist exceed 3.5 per cent of the volume billed for their meters without compensation; College Hill, Airport and East Industrial, where many of those meters are inside, do not (the air line puts them over: Airport, College Hill and East Industrial). Westgate and Heritage Park have fewer than 100 such meters.
7. The 2027 plan schedules 976 changeouts in Old Town and Downtown from April 5 to October 29, 2027. In 2024 and 2025 crews finished outside meters within days of their planned date and inside meters (which need the customer home) weeks later or not at all; at those paces 668 are done before November 1, 2027, not 976.

No other set survives. The same five districts come out with each read spread on a base-and-degree-day fit of the district's sendout, with the gas temperature tied to the commercial correctors' instead of the soil, and under the air coefficient 20 per cent either way, the indoor temperature 2 F either way, and the damage, own use and diversion volumes 20 per cent either way. Each shortcut lands on a different list: the air line on Downtown and Old Town; the 2019 consultant's monthly air on none; reads spread evenly on Downtown, Halvorsen, Lakeview, Old Town, Riverside and Sundquist; no diversion on Downtown, Elm Grove, East Industrial, Lakeview, Old Town, Riverside and Sundquist; every collapse as diversion on Downtown, Old Town, Riverside and Sundquist.
