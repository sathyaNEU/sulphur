**Put Downtown, Lakeview, Old Town, Riverside and Sundquist into the 2027-2029 main replacement program, and send the 2027 meter changeout to Elm Grove, Halvorsen, Lakeview, North End, Prairie Rose, Riverside and Sundquist.** Their leak loss in the twelve months to September 2026 was 1.17 to 1.57 per cent of their gas in (Riverside 1.17, Lakeview 1.21, Sundquist 1.35). ## Step-by-Step

1. Rebuilt each regulator station's faulty gas days from the city gate less the other stations (Riverside's January 12 to 25 communication gap, Lakeview's December transducer failure, Westgate's stuck temperature transmitter); Lakeview's December 2025 gas in is 13,536.7 Mcf.
2. Set each station corrector's corrected-to-uncorrected ratio against its pressure-and-temperature factor (atmospheric 13.98 psia, solved from the customer correctors' own ratios): the 12 EVC-II units run 1.0043 to 1.0096 above it, the gas's supercompressibility at their pressure, the four EVC-I units at 1.0000. Put the EVC-I stations on the EVC-II basis with Dranchuk and Abou-Kassem on the gate's specific gravity, which the EVC-II stations' own records reproduce: Riverside's twelve months are 128,796 Mcf against 128,005 recorded, and 3,035 Mcf across the fourteen districts.
3. Every customer corrector's ratio is its pressure-and-temperature factor exactly, so the process customers at 25 to 58 psig are billed short of their gas at base conditions: the corrector customers took 898,566 Mcf in the twelve months, 2,944 more than billed, 2,220 of it in East Industrial.
4. Turned the AMR reads into actual-to-actual periods per meter, carried them to months on each district's daily sendout, and corrected the meters without temperature compensation with the gas temperature the loggers tie to the soil at 20 inches (plus 0.181 of the air's difference from it) and indoors to the basement's.
5. Took each premise's monthly use against its own weather response the year before: 112 fell for three months or more into the heating season outside a vacancy or an exchange. 9 lost their summer base load too (bypasses). 52 were back at their own weather response in May 2026 (away for the winter). Of the rest, read period by read period against the degree days of the cold days only, 31 took their whole past heating slope on the coldest days (dual-fuel heat pumps), and 12 more have a dual-fuel controls rebate on file. One premise changed hands over the spring. That leaves 7 furnace taps: 16 premises, 18 accounts. Booking every fall instead counts 99 premises and puts Lakeview at 0.17 and Sundquist at 0.61; booking the dual-fuel homes as taps puts Riverside at 0.93 and Sundquist at 0.61.
6. Leak loss is gas in less the billed volume, the under-registration, the corrector customers at base conditions, own use, the damage reports and the gas taken without authorization: Old Town 1.57; Riverside 1.17; College Hill 0.78; North End 0.59; Westgate 0.40; Lakeview 1.21; Prairie Rose 0.25; Heritage Park 0.18; East Industrial 0.67; Downtown 1.35; Airport 0.67; Elm Grove 0.67; Halvorsen 0.83; Sundquist 1.35. The careful but hurried route (every corrector as recorded, every fall booked) leaves Downtown, East Industrial and Old Town over the line.
7. Rule 2 on the under-registration: Elm Grove, Halvorsen, Lakeview, North End, Prairie Rose, Riverside and Sundquist exceed 3.5 per cent of the volume billed for their meters without compensation with 100 or more such meters; Westgate and Heritage Park have fewer than 100.
