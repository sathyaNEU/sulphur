Four reminders from review
What reviewers send back most often in prompts, to check before you submit

Prompts that need a rewrite slow down your task. These four reminders cover most of what reviewers send back.

Write a natural request, yourself. Write it the way the request would really reach you: a forwarded message, a formal letter, a blunt question. No persona opener, no file list up front, no First/Next/Finally, no closing list of documents. LLM-written prompts are rejected.
Pin the outcome, never the method. Say what has to be decided and the threshold that settles it, so the answer is deterministic. Never name the method, the trap, the comparison group, the confounder or the adjustment, and never warn ("watch out for", "be careful", "make sure to account for"). A standard or manual that spells out the deciding calculation counts as naming the method.
Make the deep step the hard part. The models should fail by doing a reasonable analysis that does not go far enough, not by missing one hidden detail. If telling the solver one fact would make the task routine, the task is a surface stump. See Deep reasoning.
One recommendation, a few hard asks, 1 to 3 real deliverables. Two or three multi-part asks that each take analysis, in your assigned domain, and files described by who will use them.
LLM-generated prompts are rejected
Take this seriously. LLM-generated prompts are rejected, and repeated use can lead to offboarding.

The first leaves the selection problem, the comparison, the cleaning and the uncertainty to the solver, which is where a reasonable but shallow analysis falls short.
