Domains
Seven major domains, their 21 subdomains, and example task types for each.

You are assigned a subdomain within one of the seven major domains below. Each subdomain lists the kinds of tasks that belong to it. A task must use the field’s own reasoning and meet the deep reasoning standard.

Example prompts and Stumping strategies use the same major domains and subdomains.

Major domains and subdomains

Product Analytics
Product Analytics
9 example task types
Example task types
A/B testing (experiment design, power analysis, variance reduction, readout)
Metric design (defining, validating, and instrumenting product KPIs)
Cohort and LTV modeling (retention curves, customer lifetime value)
Uplift modeling (estimating heterogeneous treatment effects for targeting)
Observational causal inference
Churn, propensity & uplift modeling
Funnel & journey analysis
Network-effect & marketplace experimentation
Feature adoption & diffusion modeling
What makes it deep
experiment and cohort reads where the simple comparison misleads, such as a lift that fades once novelty wears off, users who interfere with each other across arms, a mix shift across platforms that hides opposite responses, or immature cohorts whose retention has to be projected before options can be compared.

Domain fit
the deciding step is a product measurement judgment (an experiment readout, a retention or LTV projection, a funnel attribution), not a generic ranking that happens to use event logs.

Customer satisfaction & driver analysis

What makes it deep

Domain fit
the decision turns on a marketing measurement question such as incrementality, segment response or choice behavior, not on summing spend by campaign name.

What makes it deep
choosing and justifying a model out of sample rather than by in-sample fit, handling missingness that is not random, accounting for measurement error, controlling for many comparisons, and matching uncertainty to clustered or repeated observations. Domain fit
here the field is the statistical method itself, so the deciding step must be a statistical judgment a careful statistician would defend, not subject-matter arithmetic with a statistics label on it.


Biomedical and Health Sciences
Biomedical & Clinical Research
9 example task types
Example task types
Clinical trial design (randomization, sample size, adaptive and cluster designs)
Survival analysis (Kaplan-Meier, Cox models, competing risks)
PK/PD modeling (drug exposure and dose-response relationships)
Statistical genomics (GWAS, differential expression, polygenic risk)
Diagnostic & biomarker accuracy
Longitudinal outcomes & repeated measures
Adaptive, platform & basket trial methods
Real-world evidence & comparative effectiveness
Medical imaging & reader-study evaluation
What makes it deep
time-to-event analysis with censoring and competing risks, hazards that are not proportional over follow-up, dropout that differs by arm, and uncertainty that matches a cluster or stratified randomization. Heterogeneity across prespecified subgroups also counts, as long as it is real and not one of many fishing comparisons.

Domain fit
the deciding step needs clinical trial or biostatistical reasoning about an endpoint, not just patient vocabulary on a generic comparison.

What makes it deep
standardizing rates when the population's age structure shifts, correcting for reporting delays before reading a recent trend, smoothing unstable small-area rates, and separating an intervention's effect from a secular trend or a concurrent program.

Domain fit
the decision rests on an epidemiological quantity (a rate, risk, attributable burden or outbreak signal) estimated with the field's methods, not on a list of clinics checked against criteria.

Elasticity & welfare / policy-impact estimation

What makes it deep
identifying a causal effect when the treatment is endogenous, checking pre-trends in a staggered rollout, letting effects build over time, and weighting heterogeneous effects to match what the decision counts. Forecasts that must hold up through revisions or structural breaks belong here too.

Domain fit
the deciding step is an economic causal or forecasting judgment, not a published series copied into a table.
