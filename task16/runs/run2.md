4. Assumptions and judgment calls.
Delivery-event estimates are planning estimates / evidence-supported lower bounds, not a proven census of every latent false pair. The log covers deliveries at these facilities in 2006-2026; pre-2006 twins and births outside these facilities are unobserved, and blood coverage is partial and strongly age-skewed. I did not extrapolate a twin prevalence to older records or model genetic blood-type correlation because no birth-level evidence exists for those cohorts and such a model would manufacture precision the inputs do not contain. This is disclosed in both deliverables. A sufficiently large unobserved older-twin population could in principle push A or B over their ceilings; that is the estimate's principal limitation and why the PDF frames the figures as delivery-event-evidenced planning estimates and requires re-running at conversion. It does not rescue C or D on this evidence, and it does not change the A-over-B policy comparison if both remain eligible.
Record-to-birth assignment uses my weighted margins, not a rule-independent ground-truth identifier. One record was unresolved. Misassignment could move individual false pairs but is unlikely to erase C's 9-pair excess or D's 33-pair excess; A and B have the largest headroom (29 and 15 false merges).
"Distinct patients" is operationalized as Rule A connected components, because no file carries a true patient identifier and Rule A cannot see true duplicates that fail its four fields. If the question intends independently adjudicated real-world persons, that exact count is not identifiable from these inputs; I do not present 232,623 as one. Likewise "patients still under more than one active record" is read as linked components retaining multiple active source records, not as true duplicates A failed to join.
Overlay "confirmed" means evidence beyond history itself (discordant blood with AUTO merge, or death/activity conflict), not every historical AUTO merge and not proof no other overlay exists. Counting all 24 CB blood-discordant records instead gives Rule A overlay exposure of 9 links / 9 records; adding the five death-only records gives 10 / 10; no rule decision changes.
Death notices are used as overlay/activity evidence, not as clearance. All 2,630 rule-relevant pairs where both records have notices share the same death date, consistent with duplicates but not proof against twins or shared notices.
Extracts are the planning baseline, not conversion-weekend indexes, and links do not retire source records. The overlay split scenario only reinstates retired demographics; it does not redistribute blood/delivery/death events because history has no encounter detail.
5. Alternatives rejected.
Halvard national reference rate (~1 in 10 billion for name/DOB/sex agreement): would make every rule appear safe, but it prices independent agreement and ignores twins, shared households and mother-SSN newborns; the delivery log directly exhibits those dependencies (including 12 direct C-eligible twin pairs before propagation), so it is not the HIM-14 population rate.
Direct twin-pair counting only (C appears compliant at 12 ≤ 25): rejected because conversion errors are per record pair, not per birth; propagation produces 34 C-false pairs, exceeding 25. The same expansion is what makes A/B's 3/2, not 1/1.
Pilot review as rule-rate evidence: rejected; review-band support does not cover A/B/C (no review pair meets them) and only six pairs meet D.
Every blood conflict treated as a false merge: not adopted as primary because most are better read as overlays/assay artifacts (e.g., merge-history-corroborated records, or no-history records such as CB1056483 whose death notice matches Rosario 20185685); retained only as a stress test, under which A/B still pass and C/D still fail.
Every AUTO merge treated as an overlay: rejected; first-name difference alone is not patient-level proof, and would conflate true merges with overlays. Only event-corroborated records are counted, with the alternatives listed.
Full transitive name equivalence: rejected as primary (can equate names that are merely connected through different roots); sensitivity adds one C link only.
Rule B as the selection: qualifying but joins 28,918 fewer pairs than A; HIM-14 mandates A. No rule / all HIM review: rejected because A and B meet the ceiling on this evidence. Rule C / D: rejected on excess false merges.
Evidence against the recommendation: raw blood conflicts, if all treated as false, raise A to 8 / 0.248 and B to 7 / 0.394; both still pass and A still joins more. C/D remain failures. Overlay exposure is real but small for A (9 links / 9 records) and is a split-first precondition, not a reason to prefer a failing rule.
