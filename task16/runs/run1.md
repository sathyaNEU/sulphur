Assumptions and judgment calls.

Distinct linkage patients and multi-record patients are Rule A connected components on the as-extracted baseline with source records treated as active; this was chosen because the extracts contain no independent identity spine and the task asks for computable post-link holdings. A true post-split identity census requires HIM split extracts and re-running rules; 233,159 is only a floor, not reported as the main census.
Birth deliveries are distinct patients, including twins and separate same-day deliveries; duplicates assigned to births by the stated evidence hierarchy. MATEO’s three identical records are irreducibly ambiguous; I used the one-duplicate reading, disclosed the split reading, and the decision is invariant.
False-merge estimates were not scaled from the 2006-2026 delivery log to all birth years or all pairs: that would imply unsupported uniformity. They are independently identifiable planning evidence/lower-bound risk, not a certified accuracy rate; older or unlogged twins, field collisions and address/phone movement remain unobservable.
The overlay answer is an operational split-review screen, not proof each flag is two patients. I chose it because AUTO/REGSVC’s behavior is consistent with a loose last+DOB+sex process and Different first names are strongly enriched for independent conflict, while requiring blood/death proof would discard 515 flags merely lacking coverage. Expanding to all incompatible blood screens or treating CB1286116, CB1161409 or compatible JW/Exact merges as overlays would change the flag count by a handful and Sheet 03, but not A’s selection; it would need HIM adjudication.
The historical AUTO process was not treated as candidate-rule ground truth; doing so would import a different rule’s error process.
January 1 was retained, not nulled, because the plan does not null it. If HIM instead treats January 1 as unknown birth date, A/B/C pair counts and false estimates change and the audit must be re-run.
Name equivalence was table-entry grouping because it reproduces pilot labels; literal pair lookup was rejected on evidence.
The 2 November extracts were used as §7.2’s planning baseline; I did not invent April 2027 patients from registration volumes.
Overlay splitting before production is assumed for deployment; nevertheless the requested baseline links and overlay-touch counts were computed without removing or splitting flags, and Sheet 04 says to re-run after split.
A different reasonable reading of multi-record “still” as true unlinked duplicates would not be derivable from these files; I report the record-level EMPI census and disclose this rather than manufacture a number.
Alternatives rejected.

Rule B: qualifies at 0.112 per 2,000, but §3 joins the most qualifying pairs; A joins 28,918 more. B is not safer on these labels, contrary to the intuition that an address test helps; A’s rate is lower.
Rules C and D: C’s variant-name/contact admissions produce 34 estimated false merges, 1.349 per 2,000 versus ceiling 25.208; D’s name-blind SSN+DOB joins 54, 2.521 versus 21.4175. Delivery-log twin examples rule both out.
Rule A rejected? No, but evidence against it exists: MATEO split sensitivity raises A to 5/0.155, still far inside; unlogged twins, placeholder collisions or materially invalid overlay flags could add false merges, and more than about 29 additional A false merges would erode its 29.242 headroom. Nothing in the provided sources evidences that many, but this is a limit, not proof of zero.
National Halvard weights: spec’s roughly 1-in-10-billion exact-pass claim assumes independence and is contradicted by local households/twins/SSN reuse/January 1; it cannot bound conversion risk.
Pilot review as auto-rule validation: rejected because its 19.2-30.9 band lies below all automatic A/B/C decisions and only six D-like pairs appear, all “Same patient,” 0 false. That would falsely certify D.
Historical merge false rate as A/C/D estimate: AUTO/REGSVC behaves like last+DOB+sex and its 570/7,493 = 7.61% Different first-row share measures that process, not Plan rules.
Blood/death-only overlay census: would yield roughly 29 CB records and miss the coverage-limited Different-first screen; Different roots showed 44.44% repeated-screen conflict versus about 0.07% background, supporting the broader screen. Conversely, treating every non-merge conflict as overlay was rejected as background noise.
Compatible-name historical merges: CB1161409 Santiago/Santino and MATEO CB1286116 could be twin false merges or true duplicates; not added to the conservative 536 screen, disclosed for review. This limits but does not change A.
Registration volumes/Rosario report: aggregate operations, no pair identities; rejected for linkage truth and April projection.
Capture-recapture/score calibration/threshold tuning: no second capture process or automatic-band sampling frame; would manufacture precision and depart from §3’s pair-count decision.
