3. Line 2 sections 1 and 6, washed on 17 October and 20 October, took the four days of southerly wind before the 27 October front, after every October reading: 1.9 and 2.1 times the level on 31 October. At the test insulators' average rate, or read off the latest test reading, they stay below it.
4. Read the deposit at every fog-night flashover on the Gulf porcelain: the median is 0.064 mg/cm2, and Line 2 structure 63 flashed over 5 times below 0.60 of it, after each of the five washes of Line 2, Section 4, in fog and in light rain, while no other structure there flashed. Salt flashes over only above its level; a cracked string flashes whenever it is wet. Booking every fog-night flashover as salt leaves it off the list and drags the level down with it.
5. Took the level as the tenth percentile of the deposits at the remaining salt flashovers: 0.053 mg/cm2 (0.028 to 0.064 across the lowest, the 5th and the 20th percentiles and the other fits). 13 sections are at or above it.
6. A tree does not stand still for four summers; the span carries the 12.47 kV underbuild (`structure_inventory_69kv_2026.csv`). Copying the patrol and the confirmed grow-ins' signature (hot, still, no stroke) clears the span instead.
7. Grouped faults of one cause within 400 m (procedure RP-04) and took off what was fixed after the last fault (`line_maintenance_work_orders_2022-2026.csv`, `vegetation_work_orders_2022-2026.csv`): 6 perch guards, 4 strings, 4 spans, 2 stands and the clearance at Line 6, structures 57-58.

No other list survives the files. Salt cannot flash over on porcelain washed four days earlier, so the flashovers that kept coming at Line 2 structure 63 after every wash are the string's; a tree grows, so a conductor that has touched at the same temperature for four summers and through a trim is touching something fixed; and the test insulators' gains follow the onshore wind, so sections washed before the late October blow did not stay clean because the last reading came before it.
