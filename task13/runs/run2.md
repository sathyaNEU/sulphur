What I did this step

Read all eight ESDD test sites and 439 readings, the washing sections/log, and rechecked RP-04 §2: a section is due when its 31 Oct deposit is at or above the deposit at which 69 kV porcelain line posts have flashed over in fog. The strings are the same porcelain type, never washed, and are sampled in place without cleaning—so they are deposition/natural-wash references, not measurements of a washed line section.

Fitted an exploratory daily model to changes between successive test-string samples: deposition proportional to squared onshore wind component, rain removal by retention exp(-k × rain_mm), and coefficient decaying with Gulf-shore distance. Constant-rate deposition fit poorly; onshore-squared wind improved it substantially. A global fit around 140° gave coefficients roughly 0.000437 × exp(-0.557 × shore_km) + 0.0000123 per day per mean squared onshore component and k ≈ 0.0284 mm⁻¹. RMS log error was about 20–24% across T1/T2/T3/T6/T7; T6 alone was about 16.4% and T3 about 21.3%. I did not establish the true shore-normal orientation; fits varied roughly 135–150° and T1 preferred a different angle, so this is not a uniquely identified model.

Found the critical period the October readings miss: 23–26 October 2026, a high onshore-wind, essentially rain-free salt-deposition event. Coastal stations saw mean squared onshore components around 140–236 (m/s)² on the peak days, versus roughly 17–29 in ordinary early-October days. Every October test reading was taken 5–20 October, before this storm. Any 31 October estimate must therefore simulate the storm, not merely use the latest ESDD value.
