Step-by-step process.

Locate and inventory the input. /home/agent/workspace/input-assets/3.2.zip was extracted under /tmp/opencode/sp141. It contains the regulation PDF; crossing and station registers (gully_crossings_register.xlsx, road_stations_register.xlsx); station/totaliser visit book; tips for VFO, PRD, RSC, MSO, FRC and CSR; present-weather blocks and code list; radar pixels/cell p60max; four Rio Scuro weighing gauges and their 2013-2026 events; 1991-2020 500 m precipitation normals; Ardesa Centro 1961-2026 daily precipitation; and SP 141 daily traffic. All were opened or profiled; the daily valley series, normals and traffic were ultimately not used because regulation 3.1/3.2 directs the governing station tip record and source-area point-depth estimate, not areal normals, a valley daily record or traffic. Crossing structures/widths/years were retained for identification, not for classing.

Extract and read the regulation. No PDF library was available initially; I decompressed the PDF streams and decoded its ToUnicode CMaps to recover revision 4. The operative clauses are: warm season 1 May-31 October; storm day is the day its greatest 60-minute depth falls (2.1); station storms separated by at least six hours without rain (2.2); triggering depth is point rain at the source area, a storm counts once regardless of how often it exceeds (2.3/2.4); expected storms are the mean over every complete warm season of the station tip record (3.1); the warning depth is the greatest depth reached, counted as at 2.3, in a mean of at least five storms a season over the same seasons (3.2); class thresholds and protections (4.1); monthly visits approximate season opening/closing (5.1/5.2); revision 4 moved patrol start to 1 May and added 3.2.

Read the crossing and station registers. Seven crossings map to VFO (GC-0790/SA-21768), PRD (GC-1320/SA-21704 and GC-1620/SA-21853), MSO (GC-2240/SA-21731 and GC-2420/SA-21879), FRC (GC-2990/SA-21802) and CSR (GC-3210/SA-21815). Source elevations exceed stations by 523-1,082 m; trigger depths range 12.5-29.0 mm/60 min. Station gauge metadata give TB-1 400 cm2/0.1 mm at VFO, RSC, FRC and TB-2 200 cm2/0.2 mm at PRD, MSO, CSR, all logged UTC+1 all year.

Open tips, find non-chronology, and de-duplicate. The files are not purely chronological: at several points a block ends and a later file row jumps backward to an earlier timestamp. Those jumps resolve to exact repeats of timestamp/depth records, not a clock offset requiring time shifting. I therefore sorted each file and dropped exact tip_time+tip_mm duplicates before any storm depth, event matching or total: VFO 270 removed, PRD 335, RSC 308, MSO 233, FRC 370, CSR 286 (1,802 total). De-duplication was material; several apparent tip/storage ratios above unity disappear afterward.

Check season completeness. Visit-book opening rounds fall in late April and closing rounds in November for every station-year, covering 1 May-31 October. All six stations have 18 complete warm seasons, 2009-2026 inclusive; none was dropped. This is recorded in "Records and QC". Funnel/clearance remarks, storage-gauge pollen notes, battery/card work and technician changes were reviewed; storage intervals were used only as a tip-total plausibility check, not as the classing estimator.

Reconstruct storms from independent rain evidence. Present-weather blocks (200 h/event records) were converted to UTC and grouped per station with gaps over six hours separating storms; gaps were measured from the previous block's end to the next block's start. This gives roughly 80-104 in-season storms per station-year (e.g. VFO 87 in 2009, 102 in 2023, 70 in 2025; exact per-station totals are not needed for the class rule). A storm's measured depth M is the largest sum of de-duplicated nominal tip depths in 3,600 consecutive seconds, via a two-pointer sliding window; the peak interval drives peak weather features and the storm's season (converted back to fixed UTC+1).

Reject present-weather intensity as true depth. Integrated block depths do not reconcile with tips or storage: event ratios span roughly 0.17-2.8, and intervals vary widely while tip/storage totals stay near unity. Inspecting block-to-block tip intervals showed piecewise-constant spacing tracking an event-specific multiple of block intensity, inconsistent with treated rainfall depth. So present-weather blocks were used for storm separation and for peak type/intensity, never as calibrated rainfall. Peak hail was defined as GR or +RAGR contributing more than 1% of peak present-weather depth; peak intensity is the maximum block intensity overlapping the 60-minute tip peak.

Match storms to radar cells. The 2018/2019 pixel-set change was handled with pixel_until_2018 for cells before 2019 and pixel from 2019. Cells covering each station pixel and crossing source pixel were selected per period. Cell-event times map one-to-one onto station storms (833 VFO, 772 PRD, 877 MSO, 834 FRC, 712 CSR, 876 RSC events); post-2013 non-cell storms are long stratiform events with tipping peaks around 6.0 mm and no trigger relevance at these thresholds. Per-crossing event-cell log-ratio means/spreads (e.g. GC-1620 mu 0.323, sigma_R 0.133; GC-2420 mu -0.053, sigma_R 0.144) are printed in "Estimation basis".

Calibrate station tips against true Rio Scuro p60. RSC has four weighing gauges (876 events each; 3,504 gauge-events), so I selected each cell's radar depth at both the RSC road-station pixel and each gauge pixel and paired true weighing p60 with RSC tip-peak depth in a widened event window. A pooled log-linear fit over all gauge-events estimated log(true / (M x R_source/R_station)) = 0.070209 + 0.027746 log(I_peak/20) + 0.243811 hail + 0.022965 dE_km + e, where dE_km is source-minus-station elevation in km. Residual spread was modeled as sd(e | dE) = 0.125472 + 0.112015 |dE_km|; this elevation-difference law, rather than distance, is what the Rio network distinguishes (similar-distance W2-W3 vs W2-W4 pairs separate on elevation), and it is what I extrapolate to the crossings. Calibration was checked with gauge-wise and event-split fits, and the residual law is why the final quantity integrates rather than thresholds a point estimate.

Estimate each source depth and the classing quantities. For each of the 18 seasons and each station storm: if radar exists (2013-2026), use its realized source/station cell ratio; for 2009-2012 use the crossing's pooled log-ratio mean and a total spread sqrt(eta^2 + sigma_R^2). Applying the calibrated model yields a normal log-distribution for the source-area 60-minute depth. The expected trigger count is the sum over 18 seasons of each storm's probability of reaching the geotechnical trigger, divided by 18; classes follow 4.1. Depth 3.2 is found by bisection as the d where the same expected count equals 90 storms over 18 seasons (five a season).

Checks run. Exact-de-duplication makes storage/tip interval ratios far more coherent (post-dedup station means about 0.949 VFO, 0.955 FRC, 0.963 RSC, 0.971 MSO, 0.978 PRD, 0.982 CSR, with no large >1 artifacts); event-cell matching yields one cell per storm and no source-pixel misses; Rio within-event transfer residuals and elevation dependence were examined before choosing the residual law; long stratiform storms contribute negligibly to the thresholds (about 0.11 triggers/season even for lowest-threshold GC-2240, under 0.01 for GC-0790, nil for GC-2420); per-year and period (pre/post-2019 pixel change) ratios were checked stable; a point-plug alternative (residual spread zero) was evaluated explicitly.

Assumptions and judgment calls.

The classing asks for storms a season to expect, so I estimate an expected rate, not an unknowable realized source history. Each historical station storm keeps its timing/season, but its source depth is treated as the Rio-calibrated conditional distribution, and probabilities are summed. If instead one replaces each storm by a single point estimate and thresholds it (residual spread zero), GC-0790 falls to class 2 at about 2.67-2.89 triggers/season (versus the tabled 4.09) and tabled depths drop by roughly 0.8-1.1 mm; all other classes survive that change. This is the one decision materially sensitive to the reading; I adopted the expected-rate reading because 3.1/3.2 describe storms to expect and because Rio transfer residuals are real, not cosmetic. A literal historical replay with gauges in every source area could not be produced from these records and is not what an ungauged-source estimate can claim.

Tip records are assumed to contain false repeated tip segments to remove, not true simultaneous bucket tips. Exact repeats after sorting, their out-of-order placement, and the resulting storage inconsistencies support this. Retaining them would inflate depths, chiefly long low storms near the five-a-season depth.

All station-years are complete warm seasons. Late-April opening and November closing cover May 1-October 31; tips/visits do not show a missing month. Treating maintenance visits as season invalidity would discard real record and is not supported by 3.1's completeness wording; it would not change any tabled class in checks I ran (e.g. excluding VFO 2018/2020 leaves GC-0790 near its tabled rate).

Rio Scuro is the transfer laboratory and its elevation spread law is extrapolated to crossing elevation differences, including above the Rio range at GC-1620 (1.082 km) and GC-0790 (0.838 km). If the true residual law saturates instead of rising, GC-0790's expected rate moves downward toward the plug-in result and its class is the sensitive one.

One tip-correction model is applied across TB-1/TB-2 stations. Rio Scuro is TB-1; both designs carry the same nominal bucket volume (TB-1: 0.1 mm x 400 cm2 = 4 ml; TB-2: 0.2 mm x 200 cm2 = 4 ml), so bucket volume does not separate them and no independent TB-2 truth exists in the package. Station storage ratios differ only modestly and were used as checks, not to build separate corrections.

Storm season is the season of the station peak 60-minute depth, because source-event timing within a cell is not separately given; convective peaks are away from season boundaries and long events never approach the class depths, so boundary misassignment is immaterial here.

Depth 3.2 is reported to two decimals as an estimated continuous threshold, not as an observed storm depth; the regulation does not prescribe rounding.

Alternatives rejected.

Present-weather intensity as true rain depth. Rejected: block depths and tips disagree by event in both directions (event ratios about 0.17-2.8), storage totals disagree with integrated block depth, and long stratiform events show tip peaks capped near 6.0 mm while block depths imply three-to-fourfold totals. It is a storm-typing/timing instrument, not a rainfall gauge.

Raw tip depths without hail/spatial correction. Rejected: RSC/gauge events show systematic tip shortfalls, strongly different for hail-dominated peaks (about +0.244 log at the coefficient level), and crossing triggers sit in the tail where a few millimetres decide classes; GC-3210 moves from 1.11 raw events/season to 4.91 after calibration/transfer, and GC-1620 from 0.28 raw to 5.42.

Climatological fallback (1991-2020 normals, 1961-2026 Ardesa daily, traffic). Rejected: 3.1 fixes the governing station tip record and 2.4 fixes point depths at named source areas; normals are areal/annual and do not identify convective 60-minute source peaks, and traffic/structure size are not classing criteria.

Treating all 2009-2012 seasons as unusable because radar starts in 2013. Rejected: 3.1 expressly covers every complete tip-record season; I use the crossing's pooled cell-ratio distribution for those years instead of dropping 22% of the required base.

Fixed whole-record ratio only, ignoring realized 2013-2026 cell ratios. Rejected for the radar period because the cell supplies storm-specific spatial evidence; the pooled rule is confined to pre-radar years. (Using mu for all seasons barely moves results, e.g. GC-0790 4.09 versus 4.08-ish in checked variants, but is needlessly coarse.)

Dropping Rio events/seasons flagged by visit-book funnel maintenance. Rejected after checking: flagged seasons do not shift RSC calibration means or classes (GC-0790 stays class 2 under the plug-in and class 3 under the adopted expected rate; all others unchanged), and the notes are not evidence a storm's depth is unusable.

Point-plug classing. Not adopted for the reason above; it specifically flips GC-0790 to class 2 and shaves every tabled depth by about 0.8-1.1 mm, while leaving the other six classes intact. Evidence favoring residual integration is the measured Rio spread sd(e) about 0.15-0.21 log units at crossing-like elevation differences; evidence against is that a stricter historical-replay reading of 3.1 could defend the plug-in. That is the one material contrary consideration and it does not change GC-1320, GC-2420, GC-1620, GC-2240, GC-2990 or GC-3210.
