## Final Recommendation

**Give Northvale Oat the hold treatment for 2027: it will take about 2.4 per cent of the plant-based beverage category's 2027 litres (2.1 to 2.6 however the price groups are cut, the settled level read or the base litres chosen), inside the 2.0 to below 2.9 hold band, so it stays listed with no 2027 media or sampling.** This rejects the expand reading of Parfitt-Collins (4.8 per cent: penetration 21.0 x the launch-month buyers' repeat 17.1 x buying index 1.34), which carries the launch buyers, mostly premium-price households, to every buyer and takes the Loyal Grocer wave at the share it is buying in its first weeks.

## Step-by-Step

1. Read `lcp_purchases_plant_beverages_2024w01_2026w44.csv` with `lcp_product_master_plant_beverages_2026-10.xlsx` and `lcp_banner_codes.csv`. Kept the 12,963 households in `lcp_household_register_2026w44.csv` that report from 2024-W01 to 2026-W44 (6,085 category buyers, 1,259 of whom have bought Northvale Oat), projected with each household's 2026 factor raised by 1.207 so that they stand for the 16,116,449 households every 2026 factor in the register adds up to.
2. The reasonable but insufficient analysis is Parfitt-Collins with its textbook caveats. Penetration is 21.0 per cent of category buyers. Repeat is read from the most mature buyers (households first buying in 2026-W15 to W18, 17.1 per cent of their own litres in 2026-W38 to W44), and the buying index since launch is 1.34. Together they give 4.8 per cent, the expand band.
3. Split the buyers by first purchase against `northvale_trade_calendar_2026.xlsx`. In 2026-W38 to W44 the April launch wave (820,000 households) takes 17.2 per cent of its own litres and the discounter wave of June to September (130,000) 4.9. The Loyal Grocer wave of 2026-W39 to W44 (650,000) takes 32.0 at one to six weeks since its first purchase, so neither the pooled repeat nor the weekly share is a settled value.
4. Rebuilt each household's price group from what it paid per litre for the category in 2024-W01 to 2026-W14 (terciles among category buyers, cuts at 2.12 and 2.83 dollars a litre). 76 per cent of the April wave's category litres are premium-price, 66 per cent of the discounter wave's are value and 74 per cent of the Loyal Grocer wave's are mainstream, so the waves settle at different levels even after the Loyal Grocer wave's first weeks pass.
5. Joined the purchase lines to `mra_weighted_distribution_northvale_2026.csv` by banner and week, because the Loyal Grocer listing put the line on more of its buyers' shelves in September. Read each price group's settled share of the litres bought where the line is stocked from its buyers at 16 weeks or more since first purchase, in weeks without a Northvale promotion: 20.0, 8.6 and 0.9 per cent. One level pooled across every such buyer (12.7 per cent, mostly April premium households) gives 3.3 per cent (support), and 3.1 to 3.3 at any read from 8 to 20 weeks. Each wave at its own settled share with the April wave's level lent to the Loyal Grocer wave gives 3.1, and each wave at its own recent share gives 5.8 (expand).
6. Carried every household that has bought into 2027 at its own group's level times the share of its 2024-25 category litres bought at banners carrying the line at 2026-W44 (85, 83 and 81 per cent on average), weighted by those litres grown at the panel's 6.2 per cent a year. Households not yet buying come in at their group's first-purchase rate in 2026-W43 and W44, the only weeks that match the standard's basis (every banner listing the line, no promotion, no media in `northvale_media_plan_2026.xlsx`): about 50,000 first-time buyers, each with its first purchase and then its group's level, adding 0.03 of a point. On the standard's basis that is 2.4 per cent of 444.1 million litres, 10.5 million litres, the hold band; without the first-time buyers it is 2.3, and read against all category litres 2.2, the same treatment. The quarters follow the category's 2024-25 quarterly pattern (2.7, 2.5, 2.5 and 2.8 million).
7. Turned each existing buyer's expected 2027 purchases (its 2027 share of its own litres over its usual pack) into its chance of buying at least once, and added the first-time buyers: 1,310,000 households at 5.1 purchases each.
8. The other partial routes also land outside hold. The launch-month buyers' current share carried to every buyer gives 4.4 per cent (expand), the latest week 3.1 (support) and a buying index of one 3.6 (support). The share in the weeks without support between the discounter and Loyal Grocer listings gives 1.7 (delist), because it leaves out the Loyal Grocer wave.

No other conclusion survives the files. The launch buyers' repeat describes premium-price households that the later waves are not, and the Loyal Grocer wave is still in its first weeks. So Parfitt-Collins, the launch level for all and each wave at its recent share all overstate (4.4 to 5.8 per cent); one level pooled across long-standing buyers, or the April level lent to the Loyal Grocer wave, carries the April premium mix to everyone (3.1 to 3.3); and price groups read from every week since first purchase take the Loyal Grocer wave's first weeks as settled (3.0). Lending the discounter wave's level to the Loyal Grocer wave gives 1.8 (delist), but that wave is 66 per cent value-price litres against the Loyal Grocer wave's 74 per cent mainstream, and the weeks without support before September leave out the Loyal Grocer wave and understate (1.7). Every construction that settles each buyer at its own price group's level lands in hold: price groups cut by terciles, a 30-40-30 split, fixed cuts at 2.20 and 2.95 dollars a litre or clusters, the settled share read from 14, 16 or 20 weeks, 2024-25 or 2025 litres, and stocked or all category litres all give 2.1 to 2.6 per cent (2.8 at most with the 2026 window's litres).
