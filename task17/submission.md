## Tags

**Domain:** General Statistics & Data Science, Resampling, simulation & Monte Carlo methods (sizing a shared GPU partition's allocation round under a queue-wait service level).
**Analytical objective:** Descriptive & Distribution Analysis.

## Final Recommendation

**Cap the 2027 round at 340,000 node-hours of grants, which cuts the 523,000 node-hours requested by 35 per cent pro rata.** At 340,000 node-hours the partition meets its service level (a 90th-percentile queue wait of at most 4 hours over 2027) in 89 per cent of simulated years and at 350,000 in 78 per cent; the arithmetic of node-hours available over the pooled use ratio (920,000) cuts nothing, and every input in its hurried form together (a Weibull tail chosen by AIC, a constant availability, no rework after failures, one pooled use ratio) caps at 360,000, because each understates the queue's variance or its load.

## Critical Components

1. Grants turn into use at **45 per cent** in a project's first year and **66 per cent** after it, so the requests would turn into **310,000** node-hours; hourly submission counts in January to June 2026 are **6** times as dispersed as a Poisson stream about their hour-of-week means (sessions of near-identical jobs), so the demand is resampled by session, never drawn at an hourly mean.
2. Of the run-time families fitted to jobs censored at their limits, those that reproduce the 24 to 48 hour window of the limit-requesting jobs after the May 2026 change give a mean run time of **13 hours** under the 96 hour limit and **10 per cent** of normal work beyond the 48th hour; the Weibull the AIC prefers fails the window (z 11.4) and gives 11 hours and 5 per cent.
3. 2026 lost **150, 150 and 130** node-days to outages in its three full quarters, **19 per cent** of them while four or more nodes of a rack were down together (a switch or power feed), with median repairs of 1 hour (software), **4 days** (hardware) and **16 hours** (rack), so availability is a clustered process, not a constant.
4. **4 per cent** of 2026's node-hours were redone after node failures, from requeued jobs' excess over like jobs by each project's checkpointing (not the 7 per cent of counting every killed run whole, nor zero).
5. Built on the record to June and run for July to September 2026, the simulation gives a 90th-percentile wait of **5 hours** against **5** in the capacity report; every hurried input together gives 4.

No other conclusion survives the files and the policy: the cap is the largest 10,000 step whose expected use, by standing, meets the level in 80 per cent of outcomes that carry the jobs, failures, repairs and the rates' sampling error (policy 3.3 and 3.4), and every shortcut lands higher by a different amount because it drops variance (Poisson submissions, constant availability, independent failures), load (no rework, one pooled ratio) or the tail the raised limit releases (a Weibull or a cut at 48 hours). The arithmetic of hours available answers the plan's capacity line, not the policy's.
