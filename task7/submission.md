## Final Recommendation

Fund **insulation and heating controls** in the 2026 round and **not heating optimisation**: priced at the saving the 2019 lottery showed for each application's own building (controls at how much of the week it stands empty, read off its 2025 load profile; insulation at how much roof it has per square metre of floor, from the solar cadastre and the register) and carried over each measure's life, the applications received come to €108.69, €103.25 and €193.18 of grant per lifetime tonne of CO2 against the €120 limit (insulation, controls, heating optimisation). Pricing each application at its own use's saving (€131.98 and €127.98) funds nothing and is rejected, because this year's controls applications stand empty 68.15% of the week against 38.38% for the pilot's controls buildings and this year's insulation applications have 1.13 square metres of roof per square metre of floor against 0.79 (applications weighted by reference consumption, the pilot's buildings by their 2018/19 gas), and each measure's saving rises with them. Once the offer has closed and the works are done the pair pays about €6,992,958 (€6.3 million to €7.7 million on every reasonable reading of the works), within the council's €8.2 million budget, because the roofers can finish by 30 September only about 172 (155 to 189) of the 275 insulation jobs that go ahead (294 applications at close, less withdrawals); paying every job in full, as the 2019 works were, puts the pair at €10,759,494, over the budget, and is rejected, as it would fund controls alone.

## Critical Components

1. Heating optimisation's saving falls from **12.56%** in its first winter to **1.74%** by the fourth and settles towards about 0.71%, so over its 15-year life it saves **2.15%** of the pilot buildings' gas, not the seven seasons' average of 3.79%.
2. Heating controls save nothing until about **40.5%** of the week is empty and then about 3.07 points more for each further tenth: **2.92%** of the gas at half the week empty, **5.99%** at three fifths and **9.06%** at seven tenths, and 1.46% in hotels and care homes lived in round the clock; this year's controls applications stand empty **68.15%** of the week (weighted by reference consumption), the pilot's controls buildings 38.38% (weighted by their 2018/19 gas, those lived in round the clock at none).
3. Roof insulation saves **24.05%** of a building's heating where the roof is as large as the floor, **15.09%** at half and **6.14%** at a quarter; this year's insulation applications have **1.13** square metres of roof per square metre of floor (weighted by reference consumption), the pilot's insulation buildings 0.79 (weighted by their 2018/19 gas).
4. Once the offer closes the funded round's applications ask for **€11,451,661**, but the 12 roofers quoting this year (of the 26 who did the 2019 roofs) can insulate, at their 2019 pace in square metres of roof a working day, only about **172** (155 to 189) of the 275 insulation roofs that go ahead by 30 September (294 applications at close, less withdrawals; this year's roofs average 1,121 square metres, 2019's 802): insulation pays **€5,496,863** (€4.9 million to €6.0 million) and the funded round **€6,992,958** (€6.3 million to €7.7 million), within the **€8.2 million** budget at either end; every job paid as in 2019 would be €10,759,494, and the roofers' work counted in jobs would leave room for 80.25% of insulation's grant.

2. Read the pilot from pilot_2019_responses_and_draw.xlsx (3,040 answers, 1,368 drawn by lot within each measure, 1,330 with works) and set each measure's works buildings against the owners the draw passed over, each against its own 2018/19 season, as a share of the gas the works buildings would have used (plan 4.2): insulation holds near 16.91%, heating optimisation falls from 12.56% to 0.90%, controls ease from 5.28% to 4.42%. The works buildings' own before and after (no comparison) books the pandemic, the 2022 price shock and the service team's burner checks as savings and funds all three (€91.54, €27.85, €58.56).
3. Carried each measure's seasons over its life (plan 4.4 lives 30, 15 and 10 years) by fitting the approach to the level the saving settles at: heating optimisation's life saving is 2.15%, not the seven-season 3.79%; carried flat, heating optimisation would be €100.81 a tonne and wrongly funded.
4. Read each building's week off electricity_load_profiles_2025.csv (its market locations summed under its customer number in buildings_register_2026.xlsx): the hours whose load is under half-way from the week's lowest hour to its highest are empty, none where the load never falls below three quarters of its highest hour (in use round the clock); every hotel and care home is lived in round the clock, its guests or residents there at night, so its night and midday dips are sleep and quiet, not an empty building (read off their load alone the pilot's hotels and care homes stand over half the week empty, yet they saved 1.46% from controls). Banded the pilot's controls answers by it and set works against passed-over in each band: nothing until about 40.5% empty, then a straight rise; a straight line through the lived-in band too prices controls at €119.39, and the hotels and care homes read off their load alone at €113.55.
5. Summed the roof faces of solar_cadastre_rooftops_2024.xlsx at each address (the cadastre writes str. for straße) over the register's heated floor area, banded the pilot's insulation answers by it and took each band's saving on the heating part (the use's hot-water share, which insulation leaves alone): a straight line in the logarithm of the roof per floor, from 6.14% of the heating at a quarter to 24.05% where the roof is as large as the floor.
6. Priced every application in applications_2026.xlsx (a revised application replacing the earlier one) at its own building's saving (controls at its empty week, insulation at its roof per floor on its heating, heating optimisation at its own use's), faded over the life as its measure's saving fades, on its reference consumption at 0.201 t per MWh (plan 4.4 and 4.5): saving rates 22.46%, 1.98% and 8.64%; grant per tonne €108.69, €193.18 and €103.25. No other conclusion survives. The before and after passes all three on savings the comparison shows every building made, and the budget then takes heating optimisation and controls; the pilot's seven seasons carried flat fund heating optimisation on winters it no longer saves; each use's saving, or the pilot's own mix, prices this year's controls and insulation applications at buildings that stand empty less and have less roof than theirs and funds nothing; carrying only the empty week funds controls alone, and carrying only the roof passes insulation alone, which paid in full runs over the budget; each building's own saving with every job paid, or with the roofers' work counted in jobs, runs the pair over the budget and funds controls alone. Every defensible variant of the pricing (four or eight bands, the bands as steps, the empty week read at 40 or 60 per cent of the load's range, every hotel and care home taken as lived in, insulation on its share of the gas, a two-season baseline, the last seasons carried) and of the works (the firms' work in one queue, the late applications shared by the firms' pace, each roof read off its quote, the late roofs larger by their grant, the works starting a week later) funds insulation and heating controls within the budget and leaves heating optimisation over the limit.
