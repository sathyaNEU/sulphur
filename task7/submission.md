## Final Recommendation

Fund **insulation and heating controls** in the 2026 round and **not heating optimisation**: priced at the saving the 2019 lottery showed for each application's own building use, carried over each measure's service life, the applications received come to €88, €99 and €158 of grant per lifetime tonne of CO2 against the €120 limit (insulation, controls, heating optimisation). The pilot's seven-season saving carried flat at the pilot's own mix (€86, €153 and €108, funding insulation and heating optimisation and not controls) is rejected, because heating optimisation's saving falls from 11.8% in its first winter to 1.4% by the fourth and stays there, and 30% of the pilot's controls buildings were hotels and care homes, where controls save next to nothing, against 2% of this year's controls applicants.

## Critical Components

1. Heating optimisation's saving falls from **11.8%** in its first winter to **1.4%** by the fourth and levels off, so over its 15-year life it saves **2.2%** of the pilot buildings' gas, not the seven seasons' average of 3.3%.
2. Heating controls save **10.4%** in offices, **7.4%** in shops and **6.5%** in workshops, and **0.3%** in hotels and **0.1%** in care homes, which are heated round the clock.
3. On this year's applications heating controls save **7.0%** over their life, against **4.3%** at the pilot's own mix of buildings, because hotels and care homes made up 30% of the pilot's controls buildings and 2% of this year's applicants.
4. Only **36%** of the 2019 insulation answers were in by the same day after the notice, the late ones ran **40%** larger and **8.9%** of the late insulation places were withdrawn, so the funded round pays about **€7.3 million**, not the €3,050,000 received by 12 June scaled up.

## Step-by-Step

1. Built each building's gas use per season (1 May to 30 April) from gas_meter_reads_2013_2026.csv, carrying index differences across meter exchanges, counter rollovers, estimates and corrections, sharing reads off 30 April as benchmarking_method_2022.pdf sets out, converting cubic metres with meter_points_2026.csv and calorific_values_2013_2026.csv, and normalising each season to long-term weather with degree_days_haldenau.csv as the same method sets out (the use type's hot-water share held).
2. Read the pilot from pilot_2019_responses_and_draw.xlsx (3,055 answers, 1,375 drawn by lot within each measure, 1,338 with works) and set each measure's works buildings against the owners the draw passed over, each against its own 2018/19 season, as a share of the gas the works buildings would have used (plan 4.2): insulation holds near 16.7%, heating optimisation falls from 11.8% to 1.2%, controls ease from 5.2% to 4.0%. The works buildings' own before and after (no comparison) books the pandemic, the 2022 price shock and the service team's burner checks as savings and funds all three (€55, €27, €45); the checks reached 69% of works and 70% of passed-over buildings, so the lottery balances them.
3. Carried each measure's seasons over its life (plan 4.4 lives 30, 15 and 10 years) by fitting the approach to the level the saving settles at: heating optimisation levels off at about 1.2% from the fourth season, so its life saving is 2.2%, not the seven-season 3.3%; an exponential fall to nothing misses the last seasons and a straight line runs out before year fifteen. Carried flat, heating optimisation is €108 and is wrongly funded.
4. Split the pilot by the use on each building's 2019 notice: insulation saves least where hot water takes the most gas (hotels 12.7%, care homes 12.9%, restaurants 11.3%, against offices 20.1%), and controls save almost nothing in buildings heated round the clock (hotels 0.3%, care homes 0.1%).
5. Priced every application in applications_2026.xlsx (a revised application replacing the earlier one) at its own use's saving, faded over the life as its measure's saving fades, on its reference consumption at 0.201 t per MWh (plan 4.4 and 4.5): saving rates 15.9%, 2.3% and 7.0%; grant per tonne €88, €158 and €99. At the pilot's own mix controls would save 4.3% and cost €162 a tonne; each use's saving carried flat funds all three (heating optimisation €104).
6. Drew the pilot's buildings again within each use and arm and repeated every step: 90% ranges €83 to €93, €122 to €229 and €90 to €110, each wholly on its call's side of €120; the calls flip at saving rates of 11.7%, 3.0% and 5.8%.
7. Projected the round to the offer's close from the 2019 answers measure by measure (pilot_2019_rules.pdf, grant_offer_2026_terms.pdf): 36%, 53% and 80% were in by the same day; late insulation answers ran 40% larger; drawn owners withdrew 4.2% and 8.9% of early and late insulation places. The funded round's applications ask for about €7.7 million and it pays about €7.3 million; one share for every measure, late jobs priced like early ones or no withdrawals would each misstate it.
8. Took the 2025/26 saving as plan 4.6 sets it, each works building's 2025/26 use grossed up by its measure's 2025/26 saving: about 3,300 tonnes; the seven-season average on the 2018/19 use gives about 4,200 and the 2025/26 saving on the 2018/19 use about 3,600, because the price shock, the burner checks and the 2018/19 season's own high reading left that use behind.

No other conclusion survives. The before and after funds all three on savings the comparison shows every building made; carrying the pilot's seven seasons flat funds heating optimisation on winters it no longer saves; the pilot's own mix fails controls on hotels and care homes few of this year's applicants are; and every defensible variant (a two-season baseline, each use's comparison, the last seasons carried, two occupancy classes) funds insulation and heating controls and leaves heating optimisation over the limit.
