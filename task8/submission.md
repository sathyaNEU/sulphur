## Step-by-Step

1. Brought the seven boards' files to one schema: series as a year, centres by national roll number (`centre_register.csv`), one row per entry. That is 1,102,512 mark sheet rows once the 173 rows Fennor's system loaded twice are counted once; withdrawn and absent entries carry no mark (`protocol_on_banked_marks_2026.pdf`, 2.3); a reissued moderation record replaces the earlier one (`code_of_practice_centre_assessed_work_rev2024.pdf`, 4.5); Brannock's and Fennor's samples are unpacked from their delimited fields. Every sample's mean difference (centre's mark less moderator's, Code 4.1) reproduces its outcome, and an awarded mark is the centre's mark less the adjustment. On the samples of 2021 to 2026 the mean recorded differences are Ardkeel 1.47, Brannock 3.44, Dunmoyle 2.60, Eskerry 3.08, Fennor 5.26, Gortavale 3.38 marks, and 4 to 33 per cent of sampled centres were adjusted.
2. The hurried reading stops at those records. The 2025 and 2026 samples as they stand, with adjustments netted, centres weighted by candidates and a centre not sampled in a series carried at its last sample, put the boards at Ardkeel 1.37, Brannock 3.05, Dunmoyle 2.40, Eskerry 2.59, Fennor 4.59, Gortavale 2.46 marks, which leaves Ardkeel and converts the other five down. Three things about how a moderation record comes to hold what it holds overturn it (steps 3 to 5).
3. What a sample difference measures. The sample record the moderator fills in lists the centre's marks (Code 3.3, 3.4), while the standardisation scripts are the previous series' sampled portfolios marked by the whole team with no marks on them (Code 5.2, 5.3). Joined each script (the boards' standardisation scripts files) by its source series, centre and candidate to the same portfolio's row in the sample marks: 480 scripts a board over the 2015 to 2026 meetings. The live recorded difference (centre less moderator) regressed on the difference found without the centre's marks (centre less the team's consensus before the meeting, the mean of the marks given on the script, a blank being a script that moderator did not reach) has a slope of 35.8 per cent at Ardkeel (34.2 to 40.3) and Brannock 64.1, Dunmoyle 48.2, Eskerry 57.9, Fennor 70.4, Gortavale 62.5 per cent at the others (one fit a board over all twelve meetings pooled, with a single intercept). Each recorded difference is divided by its board's share. Taking the recorded difference as the difference puts Ardkeel at 0.44 marks and carries it as banked.
4. Where a centre stands between samples. The sample years rebuilt from the sample records give rotas of Ardkeel 3, Brannock 3, Dunmoyle 3, Eskerry 2, Fennor 3, Gortavale 2 years (Carrow samples every year). From the mark sheets of the last nine series, the mean centre mark (the centre's own mark, before any adjustment, since a sample difference is measured on it) by years since the centre's last sample, each centre against itself and net of the board's year (a fit on the centre-series means with a centre effect and a series effect, each centre-series counted once), is lower after a sample: Fennor's centres by 3.2 marks one year after (2.8 to 3.3) and 1.59 two years after; the first year after is Ardkeel 1.05, Brannock 2.05, Dunmoyle 1.54, Eskerry 1.45, Gortavale 1.36 marks lower at the others. A centre not sampled in a banked series is placed at its last sample over the share, plus the level for the years since. Carrying the last sample as it stands puts Fennor at 2.94 marks and converts it.
5. Whose standard. Each board's moderators apply their own principal's agreed marks (Code 5.4, 5.5) and no portfolio was marked by two boards. `moderator_register_2020_2026.csv` holds 105 moderators appointed by two boards, in 10 pairs of boards. For each such person and series, their marks less the team's consensus at each of their two boards' meetings, taken as a difference, give the gap between the two teams (476 person-series, 2020 to 2026); each board's consensus less its agreed marks (Ardkeel -0.28, Brannock -0.50, Dunmoyle -0.34, Eskerry 0.55, Fennor 0.22, Gortavale -0.30, Carrow 0.92 marks, the mean over its twelve meetings, 2015 to 2026) takes each side to its own standard; the ten links fitted together by least squares over the person-series, with Carrow at zero, put the boards' standards at Ardkeel -0.44, Brannock -3.49, Dunmoyle 1.67, Eskerry -3.42, Fennor -3.90, Gortavale -6.56 marks against Carrow's (Gortavale's range -7.0 to -6.4). Taking each board's own principal as the standard puts the boards at Ardkeel 3.44, Brannock 3.97, Dunmoyle 4.52, Eskerry 4.11, Fennor 5.16, Gortavale 3.81 marks and converts all six down.
6. Checked the route on a record it does not use. The regulator's 2023 review (`oer_review_2023_dunmoyle_portfolio_marks.csv`, 1,908 portfolios) marked every Dunmoyle centre at Carrow's standard. On the 165 centres Dunmoyle did not sample in 2023 the review finds the centres' marks 5.53 above Carrow's standard (centres weighted by their candidates, as throughout); steps 3 to 5 run for 2023 on the same centres give 5.65 (0.12 away). The three shortcuts give 3.05, 6.69 and 3.99, and the record with each board's own meeting severity (its consensus less its agreed marks) added gives 2.08.
7. Each centre's distance is its board's standard plus its level against that standard: for a centre sampled in the series, its recorded difference divided by the share, with the adjustment applied in that series then taken off (whole marks, as the mark sheet shows them); for the others, as in step 4 (`carrow_banked_marks_by_centre_2025_2026.csv`, 2,620 rows). Weighted by candidates with a banked mark (127,621 over the six boards), the boards stand Ardkeel 3.0, Brannock 0.5, Dunmoyle 6.2, Eskerry 0.7, Fennor 1.3, Gortavale -2.8 marks above Carrow's standard for the two series together, and 1.7 as a whole.
8. Applied the protocol's 4.2: Ardkeel and Dunmoyle are 2.0 marks or more above and convert down, Gortavale is 2.0 or more below and converts up, Brannock, Eskerry and Fennor are inside. The nearest board to a tolerance is Fennor, 0.74 marks from it.
9. Rebuilt the route in each of these ways (the share with or without an intercept, on the consensus or the agreed marks, as a ratio of means on the agreed marks, on the last six meetings, year by year; the levels on six, nine or twelve series, weighted or not, or from each centre's own change; one or two last samples; the links weighted, trimmed, at the median, on the 2023 to 2026 meetings, one link a board on its shortest path to Carrow, with or without a marker's own mark in the consensus; and every pair of these that changes two different choices): all give the same six outcomes, the nearest any of these rebuilds comes to a tolerance is 0.48 marks (Fennor), and the stated ranges hold every one. Each fitted input moved by the furthest any rebuild moves it, alone and all three together, leaves the outcomes unchanged.

No other conversion survives the files. The moderation records as they stand pass every check they offer (each outcome reproduces from its sample, each joining board's team sits within a mark of its principal at the meeting, a centre's successive samples correlate at 0.65) and miss the one record that tests them: they put Dunmoyle's unsampled centres at 2.42 marks in 2023 where the review found 5.53. Adding each board's own meeting severity to the record (Ardkeel 1.09, Brannock 2.55, Dunmoyle 2.06, Eskerry 3.14, Fennor 4.80, Gortavale 2.17 marks) leaves Ardkeel and converts the other five, and is 3.45 away from the review. Beside these, further rebuilds take the share from recent meetings alone or the levels from every series on file: the share fitted on the last five or the last four meetings (36.7 and 37.4 per cent at Ardkeel) with every form of the links and of the levels in step 9 and with the levels on all fifteen series (a series before a centre's first filed sample placed by the board's rota); the share as a ratio of means on the agreed marks on the last six, five, four or three meetings (39.7 to 40.1 per cent at Ardkeel); and the levels on all fifteen series (Fennor's centres 2.85 marks lower in the year after a sample) alone and with each other choice of step 9.
