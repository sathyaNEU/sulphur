## Critical Components

1. The Brightway academies' Lexis trial was a second sitting for most students, and a second sitting ran **3.6 points** (3.1 to 3.8) above a first: at the 3 academies where half the tutor groups sat Lexis first, Lexis ran 5.0 points above Brightway when it came second and 2.2 below when it came first.
2. A Lexis sitting at the July transition day stands **4.0 points** (3.6 to 4.4) above the start of Year 7, read against KS2 through the September Lexis academies.
3. Quill's standardised scores rest on a softer 2024 sample: a Quill 85 is a Lexis **79.8** and a Brightway 85 a Lexis **87.3**.
4. The programme needs **874** students' tutor time, **£349,880**, against the provisional £339,500.

## Step-by-Step

1. From census_year7_2026-10-01.csv, take the 4,292 Year 7 students on roll at the census; attach each student's sittings from lexis_results_autumn2026.csv (by UPN, or name and date of birth where the UPN is blank), quill_results_2026.csv (through quill_roster_map.csv's admission numbers, or by name inside the site's academy) and brightway_results_2026.xlsx (by name and date of birth inside each sheet's academy), dropping the repeated export rows; the first valid sitting by the census is a completed Lexis or Brightway test or a Quill session of 24 questions or more in quill_session_log_2026.csv (the policy): 4,145 students have one.
2. Read a September Lexis sitting on the Lexis 2019 basis directly, and build the KS2 to Lexis conversion on the KS2 reading (ks2_results_2026.csv) and first Lexis sittings of the students who sat Lexis in September, by equipercentile linking with presmoothing (the July sittings kept out of it).
3. Convert Quill through KS2 (Quill to KS2 inside the Quill academies, then KS2 to Lexis): a Quill 85 is a Lexis 79.8, where reading the Quill score as a Lexis score keeps it at 85.
4. Pair each Brightway academy student's Brightway result with their Lexis trial result and read the order from the two sittings' dates: at the 3 academies where half the tutor groups sat Lexis first, the gap between the orders gives a second-sitting gain of 3.6 points; take it off whichever sitting came second and link Brightway to Lexis on the pairs: a Brightway 85 is a Lexis 87.3, not the 89.0 the pairs as they stand give.
5. For the four academies that sat Lexis at the July transition day, compare their results with what their KS2 results give through the September academies: they stand 4.0 points higher, and each July result is brought down by it.
6. Place every student at the whole Lexis score of equal reading at the start of Year 7 from their first valid sitting, converted, or from their KS2 reading if they have no sitting, and count those below 85: 874 students, 349.6 tutor hours a week, £349,880 for the year; drawing the linking samples again gives 843 to 915 students.
7. Compare with each academy's own test: 784 students in all, the Quill academies 99 short and the Brightway academies 46 over; the shortcuts above together give 767 students and £307,045, inside the provisional £339,500.

No other conclusion survives the records. Reading every test's standardised score as a Lexis score is ruled out by KS2, on which the Quill academies' intake reads 104.3 against 104.8 for the students who sat Lexis in September, while their own scores run 101.8 against 100.1; a prediction of Lexis from KS2 by regression pulls every converted score toward the middle and places 720 students; converting Brightway through KS2 instead of the trial is not the trust's link for Brightway (policy section 6), and it reads those academies a little high (the students who sat Lexis first there have KS2 results that give 94.2 against the 93.6 they scored); and every reasonable version of the conversions (the presmoothing, frequency estimation for Quill, the gain from median gaps or the fixed-order academies alone, the July level by median or regression) places 858 to 875 students, differing only by whole score groups at the cut.
