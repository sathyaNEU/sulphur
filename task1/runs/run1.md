Step-by-step process.

Located and read the supplied archive under /home/agent/workspace/input-assets; it contained Zancada’s accounts, attribution installs, SKAN 4.0 postbacks, billing, ad spend, media plans, budget and change logs, creative tests, store ratings, subscription-store aggregates, web settlements and the policy PDF. I extracted the PDF text myself because no PDF tool was initially available. The policy (v3) defines first consumer-account installs; 365-day net revenue at transaction time less refunds; VAT ES 21%, IT 22%, PT 23%; store commission 15% of price-net-VAT; web fee 3%; plan year = 52 weeks; Scale/Hold/Cut thresholds; and marginal return as every week scaled in the same proportion.
Paid-budget workbook: read both “Weekly budgets” and “Changes”. I used actual platform billing, not budget settings, for observed spend, and used the logged changes as the spend-response experiments. Active campaign schedules were inspected but not used as the channel model because plan spend is channel-level.
Accounts: 748,197 rows. Removed 115 staff and 48 test accounts, leaving 748,034 consumer accounts. Attribution had 771,674 rows; 17,572 were reinstalls and 5,905 excess first-install rows were duplicate install_ids/accounts, collapsed to one install per account; no cross-source/campaign conflicts were found.
SKAN: 300,004 postbacks. Sorted by receipt time and de-duplicated 897 repeated postback_ids; removed 1,976 re-downloads after de-duplication, leaving 297,131 new-install postbacks. I mapped conversion values using skadnetwork_conversion_values.json: bits 0–2 sessions bucket, bit 3 trial, bit 4 direct purchase. Receipt timing showed a roughly 72–96-hour delivery lag; I assigned installs to weeks using an 84-hour shift (boundary sensitivity is small relative to weekly response error).
Billing: 500,694 events; excluded 469 sandbox events, leaving 500,225 production events including 1,190 refunds. Computed each charge’s net as price/(1+VAT) × (1 − 0.15 store or 0.03 web), rounded to cents; refunds are the negative net of their original charge. For each install, summed charges whose original charge time fell in [install, install+365 days), and subtracted refunds of those charges even if the refund posted after day 365. Charges before install: none.
Latest cohort: weeks 0–51 (4 Nov 2024–2 Nov 2025), 369,982 consumer accounts overall before channel/platform splits. Android and ASA installs were fully attribution-named and used actual account billing. For the five iOS channels, attribution names only authorized installs (roughly 26.3–27.7% of true paid installs); true installs came from de-duplicated SKAN postbacks. Unnamed-account revenue was estimated from each postback conversion value against mature NA iOS account billing curves; named accounts used channel-specific billing curves. This gives cohort revenue per install, not merely authorized-user revenue.
Weekly channel panels were built for 104 weeks. The naive log-log spend regressions were rejected: spend-season base is strongly collinear with volume and gives elasticities near 1, while quality falls as budget pressure rises. Instead I decomposed historical budget into a common seasonal base (reconstructed from budgets ÷ logged change factors) and a change factor f. Delivery/billing ratio was retained as a separate historical term (actual spend ÷ budget). Response form was multiplicative power, fitted per channel by weighted/OLS log-linear regression: log installs = c + a·log season + β·log f + γ·log(billed spend/budget); log revenue/install = q + aq·log season − η·log f + δ·log(billed/budget). I chose this because the logged budget changes, not raw week-to-week CPI, identify the plan-relevant proportional-spend response; it also matches the stretch plan’s constant multiplier structure.
Fitted volume parameters (season a, f β, delivery γ): Meta 1.268/0.730/1.702; TikTok 1.111/0.783/1.716; Snapchat 1.229/0.771/1.774; Google iOS 1.277/0.651/1.747; Pinterest 1.187/0.662/1.703; Google Android 1.263/0.748/1.906; AppLovin 1.120/0.812/1.866. Quality (season aq, f elasticity −η, delivery δ): Meta −0.062/−0.212/0.012; TikTok 0.011/−0.217/−0.045; Snapchat −0.199/−0.270/−0.177; Google iOS 0.012/−0.231/0.061; Pinterest −0.097/−0.250/0.080; Google Android −0.038/−0.215/−0.021; AppLovin −0.103/−0.124/0.068.
Quality was not read from raw attribution or network-reported installs. SKAN conversion values were decoded, mature payment data were mapped per conversion value, and weekly modeled quality was used to fit the response. The NA-account conversion-value→billing relationship was checked out-of-sample by week folds; named-channel and status-specific billing curves differ materially (e.g. AppLovin authorized vs denied), so a single global CV→revenue mapping was rejected.
Plan prediction held each plan week’s own seasonality, set installs and quality at f = plan spend ÷ historical seasonal-base budget at the plan’s billed spend level, and summed all 52 weeks. Stretch used the same construction at the Stretch case sheet’s constant multipliers (Meta ×1.30; TikTok ×1.15; Snapchat ×1.15; Google iOS ×1.35; Pinterest ×1.75; Google Android ×1.25; AppLovin ×1.45). Return = Σ(installs_t × revenue/install_t) / Σ spend_t. Marginal = (β − η) × return, exactly policy §2.6’s proportional weekly scaling.
Checks: volume residuals ≈4–7%; quality ≈7–9%. Refits on disjoint 78-week windows preserved response signs/magnitudes. Event contrasts around the logged budget changes independently showed negative quality elasticities (roughly −0.12 to −0.28) and sub-unit volume response for most channels. Campaign-mix/CPI diagnostics showed large week-to-week auction noise, supporting the experimental f specification over raw CPI/spend correlation. Plan spend totals reproduce the workbook exactly: Meta €220,250; TikTok €340,780; Snapchat €319,030; Google iOS €209,520; Pinterest €109,240; Google Android €285,320; AppLovin €246,940; total €1,731,080; stretch sheets likewise.
Considered and rejected: network-reported installs as truth (partner inflation/ATT gaps); organic/attribution-only iOS installs (misses ~73% of paid iOS); a single conversion-value→revenue curve (status/channel differences); raw log-log spend regressions (season/base confounding); campaign-level two-way fixed-effects CPI (budget changes are channel-level and campaign mix is not a planner input); ratings and creative-test arms as return evidence (no billing; ratings flat ≈4.2–4.6); App Store/Google Play aggregates and web settlements for event-level billing (superseded by direct transaction events).
Assumptions and judgment calls.

Plan delivery/spend level. Media plan spends are treated as insertion-order billing figures and the response is evaluated at those billed amounts with historical budget-to-bill delivery set to 1, i.e. no budget-to-bill delivery haircut is applied; the budget sheet is used to identify seasonality/experiments, not as 2027 spend. This is the literal policy reading of “weekly spend … as the platform bills it.” If instead 2027 were interpreted as a budget target whose delivery behaves like the historical budget-to-bill ratio, effective plan scale would change and placements could change (e.g. Meta and Google iOS fall toward Hold territory and Google Android further under Hold in my delivery-adjusted sensitivity). I did not adopt that because the plan/policy define spend as billed insertion-order amounts, not historical budget settings; but it is the judgment call most likely to move the answer.
Latest cohort fixed to 52 weeks ending 2 Nov 2025, per prompt/records cut-off, rather than a rolling 365-day date window or week 52 (3 Nov); using the extra partial week would not change channel rates materially but would change the requested window.
SKAN lag set to 84 hours; exact 72/96-hour assignment mainly moves installs across week boundaries.
Conversion-value billing curves assumed stationary over the two-year record (prices fixed, CV-revenue relationships stable across cohort halves); f-response modeled as log-log power over historical f ≈0.6–1.6. Pinterest stretch effective f and Google Android plan f lie inside/near that range; extrapolation beyond it would increase uncertainty.
iOS revenue of unnamed installs calibrated on mature NA-account billing curves, not channel-specific unknown denial curves; mixture models with channel-specific NA parameters did not improve out-of-sample NA revenue prediction.
Marginal return is a local derivative at the stated 52-week plan scale, not the return on the next incremental week alone.
Alternatives rejected.

Volume is linear in spend / no saturation (raw elasticity ≈1.0). Rejected by the logged budget-change experiments: conditional on season and delivery, f-elasticities are β ≈0.65–0.81 and residuals drop from ≈9–15% to ≈4–7%. A raw CPI regression cannot distinguish budget-change response from seasonal base spend.
Quality is flat with spend. Rejected: f-adjusted quality elasticities are −0.124 (AppLovin) to −0.270 (Snapchat); budget-change event contrasts independently show install quality falls as spend rises (mostly CV-distribution deterioration; conversion-value-conditional billing is comparatively stable).
iOS attribution counts are complete. Rejected: only ~26–28% of true paid iOS installs are partner-named; SKAN postbacks recover ~3.6–3.8× the named authorized volume.
Named-iOS billing alone estimates paid-iOS quality. Rejected: authorized accounts are systematically higher-value; NA-account conversion-value curves are much lower at the same CV (e.g. CV 11 ≈13.07 € vs named Meta ≈19.54 €).
Historical latest-year Android return alone sets 2027 bands. Rejected: Google Android’s latest-year quality is 6.6115 €/install but plan spend is roughly half its historical seasonal-base budget, lifting plan quality to 7.4881 € and return to 0.9427 (Hold); using the historical scale would misplace it.
Bands on stretch or marginal return. Rejected: policy §3.1 places on plan-year return at plan spend only; stretch is evidence for release, not a second placement test. Meta’s stretch 1.2192 remains Scale while Google iOS/Pinterest/Google Android stretch returns are 1.0808/1.1611/0.8494.
Evidence against the call: Meta’s stretch return is barely above Scale (1.2192 vs 1.20) and Google iOS’s plan cushion is only 0.0861, so small model/delivery changes could move those channels’ stretch/plan classifications; Google Android’s 0.9427 Hold cushion is only 0.0427. These do not change the requested plan-spend placements under the billed-spend interpretation, but they are the least robust figures.
