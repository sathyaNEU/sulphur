# Platform review, task1 architecture 3 (the 2026-11-11 reshipped cut), returned on determinism

Pasted by the author on 2026-11-12, verbatim.

What's good:
This is a serious Project Sulfur task with a real decision behind it: which lines of media_plan_2027.xlsx to fund at 3.5 times their planned cost per start, with each line valued over its first 36 months. The data package is rich and realistic (18 shipped data files across spreadsheets and documents), and it does real work: repeated export rows, staff, gift and company products and test accounts to drop under the KPI standard, renamed channel codes to map, the Apple fee version to apply, and households to link through payment_methods.csv and customers.csv. Several of your figures reproduce exactly from the shipped data files. The Oct 2026 paying stock comes to 12,230 (Rundschau 5,076, Anzeiger 3,065, Kreisblatt 2,608, Nachrichten 1,481) and matches the controls in kpi_digital_2026.xlsx, and the Jul to Sep 2023 household values per start (paid social 151, direct 256) reproduce as well.

Your reading of the April 2025 price rise also holds up well. Using subscriptions_2023-01_2026-10.csv and price_periods_2023-01_2026-10.csv, the jump in leaving at the end of the introductory price after the rise is a price effect, not a weaker 2025 cohort. Among starts from before 2025 alone, leaving still rises after the rise, and a start-year term adds almost nothing once the price is in the model. So your 7.0 to 8.6 points for the April 2027 prices is the supported magnitude. The test responses put most of that jump on a cohort term "for starts from January 2025 onward" and got only 1.2 to 2.5 points, which the shipped data rules out. That is the kind of trap you want: it comes from a reasonable analysis that stops short.

What's bad:
The task is being returned for determinism. The committed answer funds 8 lines for EUR 80,084, but the shipped data files support a second, equally defensible 36-month value model that funds 11. The difference is how you project two things: how the newest starts will keep paying at full price, and how much later revenue the same household brings. Neither digital_subscription_kpi_standard.pdf nor any other shipped file fixes the model for either one.

- KPI 3.1 asks for "expected values, estimated from the group's own records". KPI 3.3 says the line's starts are "taken to be like the line's starts of the twelve months to the last month closed before the plan". KPI 2.6 counts value "from any later subscription by the same household to the same title". These clauses define what to estimate but not how to project it. Nothing in them tells a solver to complete young starts from their first three months of reading, and nothing limits later household value to a first return through the paywall.
- A model fitted directly to the shipped records gives a different set of funded lines. It uses the observed step-up exits with your price term, the paid social starts' observed recent leaving (41 full-price exits against 29.8 expected from older starts), and the 12-month household restart rate, which holds steady at 0.11 to 0.14 from 2023 to 2025 and is worth about EUR 50 per paid social start. Under that model SOC-KBO comes to 4.02x, SOC-ELN to 3.73x and SOC-GAZ to 3.59x, so all three clear the 3.5x cutoff. Your committed answer puts them at about 3.0x (SOC-KBO 96, SOC-GAZ 200, SOC-ELN 172 per start). This is not a case of over-optimistic survival. The rival model's 3-year survival for paid social M3E1 starts is 7.8%, already below your 9.2%. The gap comes from a household return value far below what the 2023 to 2025 records show.
- Your own Checks sheet shows the same sensitivity. Completing the newest starts on each channel's older starts funds 9 lines, and its bootstrap draws vary only the start months inside your own model, not the model itself.
- The supporting figures depend on the same unpinned choices. The Nov 2025 to Apr 2026 three-year survival (paid social 9.2, band 8.8 to 9.8), the 1,869 of 2,960 plan starts still paying at 31 December 2027, and the per-line values are all projections, and their bands exclude defensible values such as 10.4% for paid social.
- The base window is also open. KPI 3.3 says "the last month closed before the plan", and the plan is dated 28 October, which points to Oct 2025 to Sep 2026 rather than the Nov 2025 to Oct 2026 your answer uses. The effect is small, but it is a second reading the files do not settle.

What needs fixing:
- Pin the survival model for young starts in digital_subscription_kpi_standard.pdf: say how starts with incomplete histories are completed (for example, from the first three months' reading in reading_by_login_2024-01_2026-10.csv) and over which older starts the full-price leaving rate is estimated.
- Pin the household return convention in KPI 2.6: whether later value counts only the household's first return through the paywall inside the 36 months, or every later subscription, and on which records it is estimated.
- Fix the base window in KPI 3.3 to named months (for example, "November 2025 to October 2026").
- After pinning, recompute the 18 line values, the funded set and the budget, and re-check that SOC-KBO, SOC-GAZ and SOC-ELN fall on the side of the 3.5x cutoff you intend under the pinned method.
- Widen or recompute the bands on the three-year survival and the 31 December 2027 paying counts so they come from the pinned method alone.

How to fix it:
Add wording to digital_subscription_kpi_standard.pdf that turns each projection choice into a stated method. For example, in 3.3: "Starts with fewer than 36 months of history are completed using the full-price leaving rate by months since the step-up, channel and the first three months' logins, estimated on starts from before 2025." In 2.6: "Later value counts the household's first later subscription to the same title started through the paywall within the 36 months, and nothing after it." With both in the standard, a solver who builds the model carefully lands on your 8 lines. The analysis still has to be done (linking logins to reading, fitting the hazard, carrying the plan's months through the April 2027 prices), but the answer no longer hinges on a modelling choice the files leave open.

If you would rather keep the projection open as a real judgment, make the decision robust to it. Set the plan's costs per start for the paid social lines so they sit clearly on one side of the 3.5x cutoff under every defensible return and survival model, rather than at 3.0x against a rival 3.6x to 4.0x. Either way, keep the price effect exactly as it is: it is well identified by the step-ups on either side of the April 2025 rise, and it is the strongest analytical step in the task.

Ways to make the task harder:
1. Make the price effect carry more of the decision. Set the plan's offers so that the gap between 1 to 2 points and 7 to 9 points of extra leaving at the April 2027 prices moves several lines across the 3.5x cutoff. That way the cohort-versus-price separation in the step-up records, not the projection convention, decides those lines.
2. Add a real budget cap to media_plan_2027.xlsx so the answer is a ranked allocation under the cap rather than a cutoff test line by line. The ordering of SEA-GAZ, SEA-ELN and the PART lines near the margin would then depend on precise value estimates.
3. Make the paid social deterioration something to separate rather than assume. If recent paid social starts leave faster (41 against 29.8 expected), let the data support two explanations, a change in audience versus a change in the creative or offer mix in marketing_spend_2023-01_2026-10.csv, that imply different values for SOC-KBO and SOC-GAZ, with the separating evidence in the shipped files.
4. Ask for an uncertainty band on each funded line's multiple that reflects how the start months and the price effect were estimated, and fund only the lines whose lower bound clears 3.5x. This turns your bootstrap into part of the decision rather than a check.

These are general suggestions for strengthening the task, not a guarantee that it will be hard enough. Please test your own model responses and only resubmit if at least two strong models still miss the answer.
