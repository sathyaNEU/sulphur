Step-by-step process.

Located input-assets/1.5.zip, extracted its 14 files and read the policy PDF (growth_policy_paid_channels_v3_2026-09.pdf): channel return is 12-month net revenue of installs bought by the period's spend per euro; an install is the first install of a new consumer account; every charge in 365 days from install counts at transaction time less refunded charges' net revenue; plan placement uses the growth sheet's 52 weeks and plan weekly spend as the platform bills it; marginal return raises every week proportionally; bands are 1.20 Scale, 0.90-under-1.20 Hold, under-0.90 Cut.
Read the 2027 media plan: Growth channels and Stretch case cover ISO weeks 1-52 starting 4 January 2027, seven platform-by-OS channels, with the growth sheet labelled "weekly spend, EUR, as the platforms bill it"; the Brand sheet has Apple Search Ads and was excluded from placement. Read budgets: Weekly budgets is what is set Mondays, Changes logs cash/pacing changes against a seasonal base, not performance. Read daily ad spend: networks, campaigns, countries, billed spend, impressions, clicks and network-reported installs.
Read attribution, accounts and the SKAN conversion-value JSON. Attribution had 771,674 rows; keeping the earliest install_id left 765,769 unique installs, with 5,905 duplicate-instance rows removed; accounts identified 115 staff and 48 test accounts excluded and 17,572 repeat/reinstall records excluded, leaving 748,197 consumer accounts before install filtering. The account table also carries platform, storefront, tracking status and 48h CV; CV bits are sessions bucket (0-2), trial started (bit 3) and purchased without trial (bit 4).
SKAN had about 300,005 postbacks: 298,019 non-redownload and 1,985 redownload/reinstall records excluded. The config is a 48-hour measurement window with postbacks sent 24-48 hours after it closes, so true install time is 72-96 hours before receipt; I allocated each postback uniformly over that interval and split it at week boundaries. This is approximate at individual level but immaterial for weekly/period totals outside edge weeks. Network installs were not truth: mature comparisons gave network/true ratios of roughly Meta 1.171, TikTok 1.221, Snapchat 1.187, Pinterest 1.129, Google iOS 1.083, Google Android 1.084, AppLovin 1.019 and ASA 1.000.
Production billing events were joined to installs. Prices are €9.99 monthly/€59.99 annual with 7-day trial. Net revenue is amount / (1 + storefront VAT), VAT ES 0.21, IT 0.22, PT 0.23; store charges retain 85% of VAT-exclusive price; web checkout retains 97%; refunds are negative net lines. I checked refund timing: 1,190 production refunds, lag 0.218-13.504 days after the refunded charge; for mature first-year installs no refund fell outside the install's 365-day window and no in-window refund related to an out-of-window charge, so the window filter does not drop a policy-relevant refund. Sandbox events were excluded. The mature year is install_time < 2 November 2025 UTC, roughly 4 November 2024-2 November 2025; charges satisfy 0 <= days < 365. Subscription daily aggregates (App Store, Google Play), web settlement summaries, creative tests and store ratings were inspected but not used: transaction-level billing is the policy source for net revenue, creative tests only describe network-side ad metrics, and ratings/settlements do not change install-level 12-month net revenue.
Built weekly true installs: exact first-consumer Android and Apple Search Ads named installs plus SKAN fractional allocations for non-ASA iOS. Built weekly billed spend from daily spend, not budgets. Ratio r = actual billed spend / Monday budget had means: Meta 0.8694, TikTok 0.8408, Snapchat 0.8719, Google iOS 0.8621, Pinterest 0.8278, Google Android 0.8673, AppLovin 0.8518, ASA 0.8362; several channels cap at 1.10 (ASA observed max 1.0601).
Compared response specifications with ISO-week fixed effects. A naive log-log weekly model in billed spend gave elasticities around 0.87-1.15 with residuals 0.088-0.105. Adding log r collapsed residuals to about 0.023-0.040, near counting noise. The retained model is I_week = mu_week * B^e * r^k; at plan billed spend S = B*r it becomes I_week = mu_week * S^e * r^(k-e). All-104 estimates were: Meta e 0.7187, k 1.7030, residual 0.0228; TikTok e 0.8202, k 1.7398, residual 0.0375; Snapchat e 0.7589, k 1.6805, residual 0.0363; Google iOS e 0.6632, k 1.7630, residual 0.0309; Pinterest e 0.6603, k 1.7148, residual 0.0396; Google Android e 0.6948, k 1.8710, residual 0.0366; AppLovin e 0.7925, k 1.8607, residual 0.0229. This budget/delivery specification was chosen because it removes the spend-ratio confounding that inflates naive spend elasticities; a simple week-FE log-log, blocks instead of weeks, organic demand controls and campaign models did not fit better or materially change evidence. Campaign models were not used for the channel response because weekly campaign budget allocation is not observed and campaign-level residuals were large.
Delivery was not set to r = 1. For plan forecasts I used each channel's observed 104-week delivery distribution. At fixed billed spend, the install factor is the empirical mean of r^(e difference h = k - e): Meta 0.8712, TikTok 0.8519, Snapchat 0.8808, Google iOS 0.8502, Pinterest 0.8197, Google Android 0.8475, AppLovin 0.8432. ISO-week mu is applied to 2027 ISO weeks.
Quality was modelled from CV/trial/revenue rather than assumed constant. Campaign-week trial models with campaign and week/block controls gave pooled log-odds budget coefficient -0.2322; converted to rate elasticities these were roughly -0.12 to -0.17 by channel. At mature Android campaign level, revenue per trial was budget-independent while RPI per install fell about 0.14-0.19 per log budget. For the plan I used a common quality elasticity of -0.20, supported by campaign CV/RPI-weighted regressions: pooled estimate -0.2025 with standard error 0.0105, and no material ratio effect; channel-specific fits ranged about -0.18 to -0.27 but were not stable enough to justify separate committed laws. Conversion given sessions/CV was budget-independent on iOS authorized cohorts, so budget changes act through CV/session/trial mix.
Latest-year RPI: Android is exact from named installs and transaction billing (Google 6.611543, AppLovin 3.134999, rounded to 6.61/3.13). iOS non-ASA names only authorized installs; Android and ASA show conversion given sessions is source-stable but authorized-vs-nonauthorized gaps differ by source. For first-year iOS I built week-by-sessions null cohorts, subtracted authorized named counts from SKAN true counts, treated the residual as organic, and fit a mixture model for nonauthorized conversion. Direct maximum likelihood matched EM; free estimates were Google -0.0651, Meta -0.7795, Pinterest -0.3609, Snapchat -0.6736, TikTok -0.9737, organic -0.3063 log-odds, with weak identification. Rounded gaps Google 0.0, Meta -0.8, Pinterest -0.4, Snapchat -0.7, TikTok -1.0 and organic -0.3 gave NLL 29784.495 vs fitted 29784.413, effectively equivalent. Nonauthorized paying-user revenue was shifted by 0.25 times the gap (Android/ASA evidence around 0.24-0.27; null-cohort profiling around 0.236). This produced the committed latest-year RPIs in the answer table.
Forecast each plan/stretch week at billed spend: installs = mu_week S^e times delivery moment; revenue = quality-scaled RPI times installs with the delivery/quality product moment; year return = total revenue / total 52-week spend; installs/week = total / 52; plan RPI = total revenue / total installs; marginal = (e - 0.20) times period return, the proportional-scaling derivative specified in policy. I calibrated the quality baseline on first-year install-weighted budgets rather than a simple geometric budget mean.
Checks: excluding or correcting capped 1.10 weeks moved response estimates only modestly and did not flip placements; month/week-split and year-split fits were broadly stable, with Google Android e more mix-sensitive (about 0.68-0.93) but pooled 0.695 and no band change; quality elasticity -0.15 or -0.25 left every band unchanged; using fitted rather than rounded mixture gaps with the same delivery treatment gave Google 1.2683, Pinterest 1.2916, Meta 1.0421, TikTok 0.4311 and Snapchat 0.5100, so the same Scale/Scale/Hold/Cut/Cut placement; first-year realized true returns were Meta 1.1261, TikTok/Snapchat below 0.6, Google 1.3299, Pinterest 1.0302, Android 0.6248 and AppLovin 0.5816, a useful sanity range once plan Spend/saturation is applied. Every workbook/CSV value was regenerated from the same forecast code and then cross-read back with openpyxl/pandas for sheet order, filters and band consistency.
Assumptions and judgment calls.

The plan's own header says weekly spend in EUR as the platforms bill it, so plan figures are treated as billed spend S, not Monday budgets B. Delivery at a fixed billed spend is represented by the historical marginal delivery distribution, not r = 1 and not a future budget path. This is the interpretation best supported by the plan workbook and policy wording. If instead plan values were treated as budgets and actual bills varied by delivery, using the analogous budget-side moments changes Pinterest to about 1.173, a Hold rather than Scale, while the other placements stay as committed; thus this assumption can change the Pinterest band, and I chose S because the sheet explicitly says billed spend.
Future delivery ratios are assumed drawn from the observed 104-week distribution, including capped weeks; ratios are not predicted from 2027 budgets (none exist) and week-of-year is not assumed to determine delivery because same-week ratios were not repeatable across years. Autocorrelation around 0.2-0.3 does not affect period expectations/marginals because both are linear in weekly forecasts.
ISO-week mu is assumed transferable to 2027; no secular demand/quality drift beyond budget/delivery is modelled. Split-year response checks were stable enough that this is not decision-critical, though exact returns could move.
A common -0.20 budget quality elasticity is assumed; campaign estimates vary by channel/year, and a channel-specific law could move exact returns by a few percent. It did not move any band over -0.15/-0.25 checks.
SKAN installs are allocated uniformly over 72-96 hours; true postback lag shape is not observed. This can move weekly weights/edges but not period placements materially.
The mature-year boundary uses install_time < 2 November 2025 UTC; weekly SKAN allocation can put boundary-week fractions on either side. Charges use 0 <= days < 365.
iOS nonauthorized quality is not directly observable. I used mixture gaps rounded to the nearest tenth because the rounded model is statistically indistinguishable from fitted EM (NLL difference about 0.08) and Android/ASA support similar gap magnitudes; an analyst using unrounded fitted gaps gets the same bands but Google/Pinterest returns swap somewhat (1.2683/1.2916 vs 1.3071/1.2565).
Revenue per paid nonauthorized user is shifted by 0.25 times the conversion gap; purchase-CV without trial had no convincing status/revenue effect and was left unshifted.
Future 2027 installs are not twelve months old, so all plan/stretch returns are committed model estimates under these assumptions, as policy contemplates, not measured outcomes.
Alternatives rejected.

Treating network-reported installs as truth: rejected because ratios exceed 1, unevenly by channel (TikTok about 1.22, AppLovin about 1.02), which would understate CPI and inflate scale decisions.
Naive billed-spend log-log response: rejected because residuals stay 0.088-0.105 and elasticities approach/exceed 1 through ratio mechanics; adding r collapses weekly residuals to 0.023-0.040, so spend-only estimates conflate delivery/budget effects with response.
r = 1 full-delivery forecasting: rejected because actual ratios average 0.83-0.87 and plan text fixes billed spend, not budget; setting r = 1 raises installs/returns (for example Meta from 1.0160 to about 1.22, Google Android from 0.8154 toward Hold), but no evidence says 2027 delivery is perfect.
Budget-model placements with expected delivery r^k: rejected for the same plan-header reason; it would place Pinterest at about 1.173 Hold. It is a legitimate alternative only if plan figures were budgets, which they are labelled not to be.
Authorization-only RPI: rejected because authorized conversion curves are similar by sessions, but authorized revenue/conversion materially exceeds nonauthorized for weaker sources (Android gaps about 0.40 Google, 0.61 AppLovin); using authorized-only would overstate Meta/TikTok/Snapchat RPI and can falsely lift returns.
One common iOS status gap: rejected because Android shows source-ordered gaps (organic/ASA about 0.20, Google 0.40, AppLovin 0.61) and EM found Meta/TikTok/Snapchat much weaker than Google; the common -0.5 EM was about 8.36 log-likelihood worse and flattened channel differences contradicted by direct Android evidence. Unrounded fitted EM gaps were not rejected statistically but were used only as a sensitivity check; they do not change placements.
No quality/budget response: rejected because campaign CV/RPI models with campaign/week controls repeatedly show negative budget coefficients (pooled -0.2025), and ignoring it would understate saturation/overstate marginal returns.
Channel-specific quality laws: not adopted because per-channel estimates varied across years/blocks and campaign mix; pooled -0.20 is the only stable common evidence, and bands were insensitive.
Campaign-level response as primary: rejected due allocation confounding and huge residuals (about 0.38-0.94 even after r), versus channel-week aggregate fits near counting noise; campaign data was used for quality, where trial rates are less allocation-sensitive.
Evidence against the call: Meta's stretch return 0.8955 and Pinterest's stretch return 0.9289 are weak even though their plan bands differ; stretch marginals are lower; fitted-EM rather than rounded gaps moves Google/Pinterest returns by about 0.04-0.05. None changes a plan-year band.
